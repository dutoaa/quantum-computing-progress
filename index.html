Variational Relevance-Informed Sparsification of Term Measurements for Variational Quantum Algorithms

Yifeng Peng, Bin Yan, Harshal Shah, Srinivasan Raghuraman, Kim Wagner, Sunpreet Singh Arora

Repeated measurements are a major bottleneck in variational quantum algorithms (VQAs). We uncover and characterize parameter-specific sparsity in the matrix of qubit-wise commuting (QWC) group contributions to parameter-shift gradients: most absolute contribution mass concentrates in a subset of groups whose relevance varies across parameters and optimization states. Building on this structure, we introduce a learned controller that predicts group relevance from Hamiltonian, circuit, light-cone, and current-state features, then selects measurements through a tunable coverage threshold. Exact contributions provide offline supervision without being required at deployment. Across 15 molecular VQE settings, our method saves 49.6% of nominal gradient-measurement shots with a mean absolute energy gap of 5.67 mHa to Full-QWC. We then ask whether large language models (LLMs) can solve the same selection problem without fine-tuning. The results reveal both promise and a clear limitation: LLMs can identify useful measurement groups, yet without guidance, local models do not consistently prioritize the physical information that matters most. Our learned controller uses these cues more effectively. This contrast points toward a broader direction for AI-assisted science: combining the flexibility of general-purpose models with explicit physical representations, task-specific objectives, and domain-grounded validation.

Restoring quantum neural network trainability through reusable calibration

Ruizhe Shen, Xianquan Yan, Kenji Kawaguchi, Ching Hua Lee

Quantum neural networks (QNNs) rely on measured quantum observables to guide optimization, but hardware noise can suppress the parameter-dependent signals essential for learning. Here, we develop a structure-preserving error-mitigation framework that improves QNN trainability through reusable calibration of the quantum circuit's effective noise response. By calibrating a reference circuit with the same gate architecture as the trainable circuit, we construct regularized estimators of local quantum features that remain fixed throughout training. We quantify how calibration errors and response mismatches affect the recovery of both observables and their derivatives. Numerical simulations demonstrate enhanced loss-landscape contrast, faster convergence, and improved classification performance, and an IBM Quantum experiment confirms improved closed-loop variational optimization under hardware noise. We further demonstrate calibration reuse in a modular QNN comprising 25 independent four-qubit channels, corresponding to 100 qubits in aggregate. Our results establish reusable feature calibration as a practical approach to improving noise-limited quantum machine learning without repeated calibration at every training step.

Nonlinear function regression using quantum ensemble learning

Yueen Chen, Mohammad Sharifian, Abolfazl Bayat

Recent advances in artificial intelligence and quantum technologies have driven the emergence of quantum machine learning, yet imperfections in near-term quantum devices remain a major obstacle for achieving practical quantum advantage. In the absence of fault-tolerant machines, ensemble learning, which combines multiple imperfect quantum devices, may unlock new quantum computing applications. Here, we introduce two ensemble-learning algorithms, namely Bagging and AdaBoost, for enhancing the accuracy of nonlinear regression problems. While Bagging trains variational quantum regressors independently, AdaBoost employs a sequential training strategy and achieves significantly better prediction accuracy. By incorporating systematic feature selection into our ensemble learning algorithms, we enhance their predictive power for highly nonlinear functions. We further improve prediction accuracy through a feature-to-qubit mapping strategy based on repeated feature encoding. The performance of our ensemble-based algorithms improves with both the number of regressors in the ensemble and the size of each regressor. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach using four benchmarking functions with varying degrees of nonlinearity by relying on a feature pool limited to first- and second-order polynomial terms.

Variational Corrections to Strong-Disorder Renormalization in the Two-Dimensional Random Transverse Field Ising Model on Quantum Hardware

Alina Joch, Benedikt Fauseweh

Strong-disorder renormalization-group (SDRG) methods provide an effective description of disordered quantum many-body systems by identifying the dominant couplings for the low energy physics. However, the resulting renormalization-group flow is approximate and neglects correlations beyond the RG description. Here, we investigate whether variational quantum optimization can be used to correct these approximations. Building on the emergent-coupling-based ansatz (ECBA) developed for the one-dimensional random Heisenberg model, we introduce a formulation adapted to the two-dimensional random transverse field Ising model. The SDRG flow determines the structure of the circuit, while variational optimization allows the state to deviate from the RG approximation. We study the resulting ansatz numerically and experimentally on an IQM superconducting quantum processor. For a 16-qubit system, we perform full variational optimization on the quantum processor, while a 49-qubit system is investigated using pre-optimized parameters, achieving a relative energy accuracy of 89% and 97%, respectively. Our results demonstrate that RG information can be translated into shallow, hardware-compatible quantum circuits while variational optimization enables corrections beyond the underlying RG approximation also for two-dimensional systems.

Entanglement Lower Bounds for Symmetric Quantum Circuits

Davi Castro-Silva, Ryan Dancy, Sergii Strelchuk

Symmetric circuits require every symmetry of the input to extend to an automorphism of the circuit. In the classical setting, their connection to descriptive complexity yields strong unconditional lower bounds. We extend this approach to the quantum model of symmetric computation defined by Castro-Silva, Gur, and Strelchuk (ITCS 2026), proving a tradeoff between circuit size and entanglement width (the smallest $b$ such that, on every computational-basis input, the state after each layer factors into states on at most $b$ qubits each). Our main result identifies a graph property decidable in classical polynomial time that no polynomial-size family of symmetric quantum circuits on $n$-vertex graphs with entanglement width $o(n/\log n)$ can compute. The bound holds for circuits with arbitrary one-qubit and two-qubit gates and unrestricted depth, and remains valid under the weaker notion of symmetry with respect to even permutations of vertices. Our proof is based on a new quantum pebble game based on the partial trace. The resulting indistinguishability theorem transfers classical pebble-game lower bounds to quantum circuits with restricted entanglement. By contrast, without entanglement restrictions, we show that every graph property in BQP can be computed by a uniform family of polynomial-size symmetric quantum circuits. Restricting to circuits with no entanglement at all, we show that polynomial-size symmetric quantum circuits with entanglement width 1 can compute every graph property definable in fixed-point logic with counting. Finally, we give an efficient symmetric implementation of QAOA for MAX 3XOR, providing a starting point for studying entanglement lower bounds in quantum optimization algorithms.

Generative Models using Multi-layer Quantum Extreme Learning Machines

Amir Azzam, M. Pilar Casado, Michele Grossi, Arnau Riera, Sofia Vallecorsa

Quantum reservoir computing (QRC) and the closely related quantum extreme learning machine (QELM) paradigm exploit the complex dynamics of an uncontrolled quantum system for machine learning, offering an alternative to variational quantum algorithms whose training is hindered by barren plateaus and costly circuit optimization. Because the quantum system itself is left fixed, learning reduces to fitting a linear readout of a set of measured observables, and no gradient is ever taken through the quantum device. In this work we repurpose this paradigm for generative modeling: a fixed, randomly initialized multi-layer QELM transforms a scalar latent variable into a rich vector of measured expectation values, and only a simple linear readout is trained to map these to samples of a target distribution. The readout is fitted by minimizing the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) between generated and target samples. We show that this algorithm is capable of reproducing a range of one- and two-dimensional distributions. Moreover, encoding the class label on dedicated qubits enables conditional generation of different classes of distributions with the same linear readout. We show that this cannot be achieved by the linear readout alone, and that the reservoir dynamics play a role in the ability to distinguish the different classes of distributions. We also show that using a simple six-qubit reservoir one is able to generate handwritten digits from the Digits and MNIST datasets with high accuracy. Moreover, replacing the Haar-random dynamics by time evolution under several physical Hamiltonians leaves the performance unchanged, provided the evolution spreads the input across the register. Finally, we show that a classical random Fourier feature model of the same size performs equally well: at the sizes we study, the QELM offers no advantage over its classical counterpart.

Teleportation of Logical Qubits using Lattice Surgery with Superconducting Circuits

Luca Hofele, Yongxin Song, Áron Márton, Ian Hesner, Ilya Besedin, Jakob Ekert

Demonstrating a quantum advantage when solving large-scale computational problems on a quantum computer requires the correction of errors that occur due to limitations in control accuracy and coherence. Using the concept of quantum error correction, fault-tolerant logical qubits can be realized and manipulated with logical single- and two-qubit gates. In this paper, we experimentally analyze fault-tolerant lattice-surgery, which is a key primitive that enables operations between logical qubits realized in the surface-code. Using this primitive, we demonstrate the teleportation of logical states between two distance-three logical qubits realized in a superconducting circuit composed of 41 physical qubits. Throughout this process, we study the step-by-step transfer of logical information from a source to a target logical qubit by characterizing the time evolution of logical observables. We find that repeated execution of stabilizer measurements, after merging two surface-code patches, restores fault tolerance and yields increased fidelity of the teleported state. When optimizing the protocol in terms of gate count and sequence duration, we observe further improvements in fidelity. Our work offers new insights into the optimization of fault-tolerant lattice-surgery operations, while also providing a comprehensive description of the physical device and gate sequences used. We expect the efficient implementation of increasingly complex logical circuits to enhance the performance of future fault-tolerant quantum computers.

Rate-1/2 Quantum LDPC Codes for Multi-tier Superconducting Hardware

Jiaxuan Zhang, Zhao-Yun Chen, Jia-Ning Li, Tian-Hao Wei, Qing-Yang Hou, Wei-Cheng Kong

Quantum error correction is essential for scalable quantum computing, while its resource overhead is governed in large part by the encoding rate, namely the number of logical qubits encoded per physical data qubit. Recent quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) constructions have entered the ultra-high-rate regime with encoding rates above 1/2, but their syndrome-extraction circuits generally rely on reconfigurable connectivity or long-range qubit transport, making them difficult to implement on fixed-connectivity superconducting hardware. Here we introduce Armillary codes, a family of ultra-high-rate qLDPC codes whose algebraic structure is generated by native iSWAP routing under hardware constraints. The construction jointly enforces stabilizer commutation and correct parallel syndrome extraction, while admitting radial and circumferential couplings in crossing-free routing layers. We construct explicit codes with rates above 1/2, stabilizer weight 12, Tanner-graph girth 6, and distances from 12 to 16, with layouts on 5 or 6 hardware tiers. Compared with the Gross and Mitten codes at the same distances, the Armillary codes achieve encoding rates about 6 and 2.5 times those of the Gross and Mitten codes, respectively, while maintaining lower hardware-complexity scores. In circuit-level simulations, the \([\![8688,4348,16]\!]\) code approaches the teraquop regime, achieving a logical error rate of \(1.62\times10^{-12}\) per logical qubit per round at a physical error rate of 0.1\%. These results show that the rate-\(1/2\) regime of qLDPC codes can be extended from reconfigurable platforms to superconducting hardware, providing a route toward low-overhead fault-tolerant quantum processors.

Quantum computing requires compilers to translate algorithms represented in the quantum circuit model into unitary operations supported by physical devices. Limited coherence time makes physical execution latency an important compilation objective. Gate fusion can shorten pulses, but gate count and depth do not capture fused targets' control demands. Evaluating every candidate with quantum optimal control (QOC) would embed pulse optimization in structural search. We present Schmidt-guided Compilation for Optimized Pulse Execution (SCOPE), driven by an operator Schmidt time surrogate. The target's angular distance from product operators measures the required nonlocal change; dividing it by a coupling-limited evolution-rate bound gives a lower bound on exact implementation time. Trace-angle and waveform constraints complete block scores for critical-path evaluation during genetic search. Local QOC determines final pulse durations. Under a numerical nearest-neighbor control model, all 104 results across 13 instances from four application families have shorter QOC critical-path durations than an adapted efficient pulse generation framework with advanced synthesis for quantum circuits (EPOC). Using eight-run instance means, the equally weighted reduction is 42.50\%, with a geometric mean speedup of 2.00. In a shared six-qubit quantum Fourier transform (QFT) ablation, time-surrogate guidance reduces mean QOC duration by 22.21\% relative to gate-count guidance and 21.01\% relative to depth guidance, validating the surrogate's joint use in fitness evaluation and mutation-region selection.

Perturbative Recovery of Stabilizer-Subspace Correlations for Quantum Computational Chemistry

Qianjun Yao, Jie Liu, He Li

Stabilizer-based subspace methods can substantially reduce the quantum resources required for electronic structure calculations, but the resulting projection neglects Hamiltonian couplings to configurations outside the retained subspace. In this work, we introduce Stabilizer-Subspace Perturbation Theory (SSPT) to recover the associated correlation contributions, using the Contextual Subspace Variational Quantum Eigensolver (CS-VQE) as a representative application. The central idea is to exploit the Pauli terms eliminated by stabilizer projection as perturbative operators that generate an external manifold from the optimized reference state. Their energetic contributions are evaluated using an Epstein-Nesbet-type second-order correction or a resolvent-based formulation that retains couplings within the external space. By exploiting the factorized structure of the stabilizer-constrained reference state, the required matrix elements can be evaluated through Pauli expectation values on the reduced contextual register without explicitly reconstructing the full-space wave function. Molecular benchmarks demonstrate substantial reductions in projection-induced energy errors, allowing chemical precision to be achieved with fewer contextual qubits in several cases. These results establish stabilizer-guided perturbation theory as a promising strategy for improving the accuracy of resource-efficient quantum chemistry calculations.

Quantum Information Transfer in Molecular Polaritonic Networks: A Van Vleck Effective-Hamiltonian Approach

Saad Yalouz, Vincent Pouthier

Achieving high-fidelity transfer of an arbitrary quantum state between spatially separated qubits is a central challenge for reliable quantum hardware. We address this question within a polaritonic framework by introducing Projected-Block Van Vleck Perturbation Theory (PB-VVPT). Based on quasidegenerate perturbation theory, this approach describes a molecular network coupled to an optical cavity and local phonons through a generalized Holstein--Tavis--Cummings Hamiltonian. Quantum information is encoded in a coherent superposition of exciton-cavity states, while phonons constitute a weakly coupled environment. In the nonadiabatic regime of weak exciton-phonon coupling, the method incorporates phonon-induced renormalization of polaritonic states and their dynamics while reducing the Hamiltonian to independent fixed-phonon-number sectors and coupled polaron-polariton subblocks. This structure provides efficient access to the coherence and population propagators governing quantum-state transfer without invoking a Markovian approximation. We benchmark the approach against exact dynamics for a molecular wheel network, considering both molecule-to-molecule and molecule-to-cavity transfer. PB-VVPT accurately reproduces short- and long-time transfer dynamics, including thermal decoherence and fidelity revivals consistent with non-Markovian memory effects, with prediction errors below $1\%$ over most of the simulated time intervals. This accuracy is achieved with an estimated diagonalization cost more than two orders of magnitude lower than that of the exact reference calculations. These results establish PB-VVPT as an accurate and computationally efficient approach to quantum information transfer in molecular polaritonic networks.

Resolving and Suppressing Temporally Correlated Noise in Quantum Error Correction

Alec Eickbusch, Nick Noll, Michael Newman, Matt McEwen, Leigh S. Martin, Alexis Morvan

Scaling fault-tolerant quantum error correction (QEC) requires suppressing errors in both space and time, yet standard logical memory experiments primarily probe spatial faults. Here, we use a 105-qubit Willow superconducting quantum processor to realize and scale the surface code stability experiment, which is alternatively sensitive to temporal error chains. Scaling from patch sizes of $3 \times 3$ to $7 \times 7$, we realize a timelike block suppression factor of $μ_T = 1.47 \pm 0.25$, demonstrating the ability to maintain temporal error suppression as the number of qubits increases. By extending the stability experiment for many cycles, we uncover a sum-of-exponentials behavior in error suppression caused by a temporally correlated error: data-qubit heating to the third excited state $|3\rangle$. We fully capture this effect with a parameter-free, device-level leakage model incorporating higher-state transport during syndrome extraction cycles. To suppress this error, we develop and realize a walking stability circuit, reaching a logical error probability floor of $\le 3.20\times 10^{-8}$ at 60 cycles---two orders of magnitude below the standard construction---and limited by ionizing radiation error bursts. Finally, by adaptively terminating syndrome extraction based on decoder confidence in an offline analysis, we reduce spacetime logical error by 38% and time overhead by 34% compared to the optimal fixed-cycle strategy.

Benchmarking memory and nonlinear processing capacity of quantum recurrent neural networks for time series prediction

José Daniel Viqueira, Andrés Gómez, David Mera

Quantum machine learning has emerged as a promising approach to time series prediction. While quantum reservoir computing has been widely explored for this setting, variational quantum models tailored to sequential data remain comparatively understudied, largely because they are difficult to train. In this work, we present a rigorous benchmark of variational quantum recurrent neural networks (QRNNs) on time series prediction tasks, focusing on two key capabilities: memory and nonlinear processing. We introduce a task-driven methodology that quantifies model capacity, enabling an intuitive and fair comparison across architectures, including classical recurrent baselines. We evaluate the proposed framework on two generic benchmark tasks and on a synthetic dataset describing a chaotic system. Overall, the quantum models achieve performance comparable to their classical counterparts, but classical models scale better with the memory size within the range studied. Notably, one QRNN variant shows higher average capacity on the task designed to jointly probe memory and nonlinear capacity. Moreover, our results show that quantum models offer better capacity when we are able to find a good parameter initialization. However, the increasing sensitivity to initialization leads to a lower probability of finding good solutions as the model size increases. These results provide a principled starting point for designing richer quantum recurrent architectures and for assessing where they may offer practical benefits.
