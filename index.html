Adiabatic training of VQAs for entanglement detection

E. Acosta, C. Cano, D. Manzano, G. Botella

This paper introduces a hybrid quantum framework for training Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs) aimed at addressing the fundamental problem of entanglement detection using adiabatic quantum computing. This problem is of foundational significance in quantum information science and has been approached through both classical and quantum methodologies. We propose an adiabatic-inspired optimization strategy for training VQAs, which combines a discretization of the parameter space with a hierarchical segmentation scheme. The optimization problem is formulated as a Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) model to leverage the capabilities of quantum annealing. Preliminary results are compared against classical neural networks and conventional VQA training techniques. The findings aim to stimulate further investigation and discussion within the scientific community regarding the efficacy and applicability of the proposed approach.

Fast and accurate neural triage decoding of ultra-high-rate quantum LDPC codes

Hong-Ye Hu, Zhenlai Wang, Binzhao Luo, Tao Xiang, Chen Zhao

Quantum error correction is essential for fault-tolerant quantum computing. High-rate quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes have emerged as a promising approach to reducing the substantial physical qubit overhead of quantum error correction, enabling encoding rates exceeding $1/2$ in blocks of approximately one thousand physical data qubits. However, their practical implementation requires decoders that combine high accuracy with low latency to keep pace with quantum hardware. Existing decoders of high-rate qLDPC codes can achieve high accuracy but often incur substantial computational latency, whereas neural network decoders have yet to be demonstrated for high-rate qLDPC codes. Here, we introduce \emph{neural triage decoding}, a framework that uses syndrome consistency to adaptively allocate neural computation. A local neural message-passing network rapidly predicts error corrections, additional iterations reduce residual syndrome violations, and a rarely invoked diversification stage searches for consistent corrections to the remaining shots. We demonstrate that the \texttt{Triage} decoder can decode ultra-high-rate qLDPC codes encoding a few hundred logical qubits, including lifted-product and Kasai codes, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy comparable to the near optimal algorithmic decoders while reducing decoding latency by approximately one to two orders of magnitude. Our framework is inspired by a rigorous analysis of the exact bitwise marginal decoder, revealing a distance-independent syndrome-inconsistent error floor. In addition, we show the robustness of neural network decoders to mismatched error priors. Our results establish adaptive neural decoding as a promising route towards fast, accurate, and scalable quantum error correction in the near term.

Digital-Analog Quantum Neural Network with Long-Range Interactions

Zongyuan Ge, Daili Li, Zewen Zhang

Protocols combining single-qubit digital operations with analog quantum evolutions bypass large numbers of two-qubit entangling gates, offering a promising approach to variational algorithms and hybrid quantum simulation. Focusing on the digital-analog quantum neural network (DA-QNN) as a representative framework, this work demonstrates that incorporating long-range interactions enhances the efficiency of such hybrid algorithms. Within the analog blocks, an Ising model with power-law interactions is employed to generate entanglement, and this protocol is benchmarked across supervised and unsupervised learning tasks. Beyond discussing the effects of the transverse field and evolution time, the primary analysis focuses on how the interaction range governs the expressibility and trainability of the ansatz. For supervised image classification and many-body phase detection, long-range ansatzes match the performance of their short-range counterparts with fewer DA-QNN layers or shorter evolution times, owing to the rapid information propagation across the system enabled by nonlocal couplings. This resource reduction alleviates the classical optimization overhead and eases experimental implementation. In unsupervised phase detection, although the long-range ansatz does not outperform its short-range counterpart in every regime, the two ansatzes provide complementary signatures that jointly reconstruct the complete phase diagram. These findings establish long-range interacting platforms as a compelling resource for advancing practical, large-scale digital-analog quantum computing.

Electronic-Structure-Informed Many-Body Encoding for Quantum Machine Learning

Liangnuo Zhang, Tanner Culpitt, Qiwei Yin, Linghua Zhu, Xiaosong Li

Data encoding is a central challenge in quantum machine learning (QML), as it determines how classical information is mapped onto quantum states and influences the expressive power and efficiency of parameterized quantum circuits. Here, we introduce a physics-informed quantum encoding strategy that exploits the intrinsic many-body structure of electronic systems to establish a direct correspondence between the body order of input descriptors and the locality of quantum gates. Specifically, $n$-body electronic descriptors obtained from a single mean-field electronic-structure calculation are encoded through $n$-qubit gates, yielding a systematic hierarchy of quantum circuits with increasing many-body expressivity. Using reduced-density-matrix descriptors, we construct quantum machine-learning models incorporating one- through four-body encodings and benchmark their performance in predicting electron-correlation energies along bond-stretching potential energy surfaces. We demonstrate that increasing the body order of the encoding systematically improves prediction accuracy toward the full configuration interaction limit. Remarkably, disconnected many-particle densities constructed from lower-order electronic information achieve accuracy comparable to explicitly correlated two-particle densities in key cases, providing a computationally inexpensive route to higher-body quantum encodings. These findings establish a connection between the many-body structure of classical data and the design of quantum feature maps, offering a systematic, physically interpretable framework for enhancing the expressivity and predictive performance of quantum machine-learning circuits.

Hybrid Quantum Algorithms for Trade Surveillance Analytics

Bimal Mehta, Daniel musgrove, Devanshu Shekhar, Shiplu Sarkar, Vanshaj Kerni, Varun Puram

Anti-money laundering (AML) and trade surveillance remain largely manual: rule-based monitoring raises alerts of which, by estimate, over 95% are false positives, each reviewed by a human analyst. We benchmark four quantum and hybrid classifiers against classical baselines on a synthetic pump-and-dump detection task: a fidelity-based quantum k-nearest-neighbours classifier (QKNN) with clustering-based routing; a quantum support vector machine (QSVM) on a one-body projected quantum kernel; a 20-qubit quantum neural network (QNN) with a Pauli-ZZ feature map and RealAmplitudes ansatz; and a hybrid quantum neural network (HQNN) in which a small classical network generates every rotation angle of a five-qubit circuit. Baselines are logistic regression, XGBoost, one- and three-hidden-layer neural networks, and classical k-nearest neighbours. All models share one stratified split of 1000 accounts (800 train, 200 test, 20% suspicious) and two 20-feature representations (ANOVA and PCA); QKNN and QSVM are also tested under simulated noise, and the HQNN is run on the ibm_fez processor. The best model is classical XGBoost on ANOVA features, at a suspicious-class F1 of 61.4%. The HQNN is the strongest quantum-hybrid model, at 54.5-57.9% F1 on an ideal simulator and 55.4-59.8% on ibm_fez, ahead of six of eight classical configurations; at a 15% false-positive rate, half its alerts are genuinely suspicious. Versus the QNN (42.0% F1), it raises under half the false alarms at similar recall with a quarter of the qubits. The QKNN reaches 43.5% F1 (44.4% under noise), above classical KNN (35.6%); the QSVM does not exceed 41%. We observe no quantum advantage, but a five-qubit hybrid on today's noisy hardware already matches well-tuned classical models, with room to grow via richer circuits and larger devices. We contribute a like-for-like comparison with classical models on shared data, splits and features.

Overcoming the Representational Limitations of Amplitude Encoding for Quantum Neural Networks

Vincent Gurgul, David Prinz

Quantum Neural Networks (QNNs) offer a framework for learning functions from classical data, but their expressive power depends critically on how inputs are encoded into quantum states. Angle encoding can require resources that scale with the number of features, whereas amplitude encoding represents $d$ features using only $\mathcal{O}(\log_2 d)$ qubits. This compactness comes at a representational cost: normalization makes conventional amplitude encoding invariant to nonzero real rescaling of the input, up to an unobservable global phase. We show that this invariance induces an exact, architecture-independent restriction on the resulting hypothesis class. No downstream quantum circuit can distinguish inputs differing by nonzero real scaling. We develop a general condition under which deterministic preprocessing can remove this ambiguity and show that nonlinearity alone is insufficient. Based on this analysis, we introduce a fixed nonlinear feature map that provably distinguishes all real inputs under subsequent amplitude encoding, without adding trainable parameters and while retaining logarithmic qubit scaling. We evaluate the resulting QNN on three canonical classification benchmarks and a nonlinear synthetic dataset against conventional amplitude and angle encoding, as well as a classical neural network. Across these tasks, the proposed encoding consistently improves classification accuracy over both quantum encoding baselines by 21$-$44 %. Our results establish normalization-induced scale invariance as a concrete representational bottleneck of amplitude encoding and demonstrate that it can be removed while preserving a compact quantum representation, marking a step toward quantum models capable of capturing arbitrary relationships in classical data.

Variational Programs with Open Clocks

Javaria Ghafoor, Amr Sabry

Variational quantum algorithms leave the durations of Hamiltonian evolutions open for a classical optimizer to set. We present VHamilton, a minimal extension of the Hamilton quantum programming language in which such durations are open clocks: one program denotes a family of Hamiltonian trajectories, and a valuation fills its clocks with exact dyadic times, so that each instance is an ordinary Hamilton program that compiles to circuits as before. Because the semantics stays exact and affine in the clocks, the Hamiltonian structure of a variational program remainsavail able for reasoning about every valuation at once. Equivalence becomes a finite calculation, which we mechanize in Agda for a fragment and use to prove the VHamilton form of the compiler rewrite "rzz(t) = cx; rz(t); cx" for every clock value. We then show what the same structure supports in a two-qubit chemistry eigensolver written in VHamilton and run through a Haskell prototype. It supports a fusion of commuting blocks, a closed form and an expressibility proof for the ansatz, and an exact split of the error between the precision of the target and that of the clocks, with a priori bounds. It also supports a coordinate search whose queries per sweep, fixed by the spectra of the generators, do not grow with the precision. On the benchmark this search reaches the numerically verified optimum of a grid of 134 million valuations in 53 energy queries. It finds the grid optimum in only 21 of 30 validation settings, however, so the gain is conditional, not a global-optimization guarantee.

Quantum machine learning geospatial time-series by seasonal encoding

Devjyoti Tripathy, Reece Robertson, Josey Stevens, Catherine Nakalembe, Sebastian Deffner

Quantum data reuploading models can increase the expressive capacity of variational quantum models, but do not necessarily improve predictive performance. In the present work, we investigate this behavior for seasonal time series forecasting and show that data encoding determines how well temporal structures are represented as circuit depth increases. We develop a fidelity-based quantum geometric description in which repeated encodings contribute to an effective input generator, so that reuploading depth changes the quantum geometry through which approximately recurrent inputs are distinguished. We construct fixed and adaptive seasonal encodings that represent observation magnitude and seasonal phase in distinct Bloch-sphere coordinates. We evaluate these encodings for monthly precipitation derived from ERA5-Land, a global reanalysis dataset, from a representative semi-arid, drought-prone tropical region in East Africa, using a 14-month lag window and reuploading depths $L$= 1-6. We find that in ideal simulations, standard value-based encodings degrade markedly with depth, whereas adaptive seasonal encodings preserve temporal correlation, spectral structure, and forecasting accuracy. Noisy simulations show that this encoding hierarchy persists at shallow and intermediate depths, while larger depths are limited by circuit noise and optimization difficulty. The seasonal quantum models remain competitive with established classical forecasting methods (ARIMA, random forests, and LSTMs). These results identify structure-aware quantum data encoding as a general strategy for building deeper quantum reuploading models.

FaultScope: A software package for fast error attribution in quantum error correction

Yuguo Shao, Zhenyu Chen, Ningfeng Wang, Song Cheng, Zhaohui Wei, Zi-Wen Liu

We introduce FaultScope, a classical simulator for quantum error correction circuits, implemented in Rust with a Python interface. FaultScope estimates logical error rates and their derivatives with respect to component error rates from the same sampled and decoded shots, without modifying or differentiating through the decoder. These derivatives enable error attribution to individual noisy components, helping identify noise hotspots for targeted noise reduction. FaultScope supports forward simulation of error propagation through circuits and direct sampling of detector and logical observable outcomes from detector error models. In our noiseless and noisy benchmarks, FaultScope has the lowest runtime among the tested simulators at every size. Computing all derivatives adds only 0.33--0.97\% to the runtime of logical error rate estimation across five repetition code sizes with 7--2047 error locations. FaultScope can be used to benchmark large-scale quantum error correction schemes and guide targeted noise reduction efficiently, thereby facilitating the design and optimization of fault-tolerant quantum computing architectures.

Quantum Bernstein--Kolmogorov--Arnold approximation

Sviatoslav V. Dzhenzher

This paper addresses the expressivity and representation gap in Quantum Neural Networks by establishing a quantum analogue of the classical Kolmogorov--Arnold representation theorem. We propose a constructive approximation framework over fixed input coherent states, where the dependence of the function argument is embedded entirely in coordinate qubits, while the target function dictates a single outer unitary evolution. By applying a Sprecher-type representation to the rotation angle of the state, we demonstrate that the multidimensional operator factorises into \(2d+1\) commuting universal inner observables. We prove that the resulting measurement probability precisely reproduces the multidimensional Bernstein polynomial of the target function. For any continuous multivariate function, the joint quantum state converges uniformly to the target output register, linking classical superposition theory with probabilistic and coherent quantum circuits.
