QASPA-MaxCut on Unweighted Graphs: Edge Encoding and QAOA Restarts

Dmytro Sapozhnyk

Max-Cut is a common testbed for near-term quantum optimization because it has a simple Ising Hamiltonian, established classical baselines, and a parameterized quantum circuit whose performance depends on the outer classical optimizer. This paper develops QASPA-MaxCut, a shift-phase circuit for Max-Cut that encodes graph edges through controlled $R_y$ rotations of the form $\mathrm{CX}_{ij}R_y(θ_{ij})\mathrm{CX}_{ij}$. We derive the exact two-qubit algebra of the edge block, trace an unweighted four-vertex example, and compare one-layer QASPA-MaxCut with depth-one QAOA by exact state-vector simulation. The protocol uses seed 42, evaluates connected backbone-augmented random graphs with additional-edge probabilities $p_{\mathrm{edge}}\in\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7\}$, and adds nineteen custom unweighted topologies. The QASPA-MaxCut score is reported after the algorithm's classical selection stage: the four leading QASPA bit strings are evaluated by the Max-Cut objective, and the best cut is retained. The QAOA score is the expected approximation ratio after local optimization, reported as a best-of-$k$ curve over one to ten restarts. In these small exact simulations, fixed-angle QASPA-MaxCut produces reported group-mean selection ratios between $0.955$ and $1.000$, the topology-level readout is unchanged in the retained finite-shot checks under the rule $\lceil 0.03047\cdot 2^n+1016.2\rceil$, and depth-one QAOA improves with additional classical restarts while remaining lower than the QASPA selection score in the reported mean summaries. This comparison motivates further study of the classical search budget needed to obtain competitive Max-Cut samples at shallow circuit depth.

Resource-efficient formulations of the Knapsack problem for quantum computing

Pim van den Heuvel, Vrinda Mehta, Dennis Willsch, J. A. Montanez-Barrera, Kristel Michielsen

Quantum computing has emerged as a new tool for solving combinatorial optimization problems. Quantum optimization approaches usually operate on the subclass of Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problems. Many optimization problems can be reformulated in this form, though there are often multiple ways to do so. The choice of formulation can considerably affect the performance of QUBO solvers in practice. In this work, we study QUBO formulations of the Knapsack problem, a canonical example of a problem with inequality constraints. In the standard formulation these constraints are converted into penalty terms, which requires additional slack variables. Here, we investigate three more resource-efficient approaches for formulating the Knapsack as a QUBO. The first two modify the standard formulation, either by deriving a smaller penalty constant or by reducing the number of slack variables. The third approach is a wholly different method, which avoids the use of slack variables entirely. All three approaches increase the probability of finding the optimal solution, both in simulations of the linear ramp Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (LR-QAOA) and on a D-Wave quantum annealer. The slackless method performs best, increasing success probabilities by several orders of magnitude even for relatively small problem sizes.

Quantum Knowledge Distillation: State-of-the-art, Applications and Open Problems

Aldana Al-Sulaiti, Kuan-Cheng Chen, Muhammad Bilal Akram Dastagir, Ahmed Farouk, Saif Al-Kuwari

Knowledge distillation transfers predictions, representations, or other supervisory information between learning models and is increasingly investigated in quantum machine learning. Its applications extend beyond compression to cross-architecture transfer, circuit approximation, distributed learning, and classical deployment of models trained using quantum-derived supervision. This survey reviews these approaches using two complementary classification axes: teacher-student configuration and the role of quantum resources in the distillation process. Quantum-inspired methods are discussed separately from methods that simulate quantum computations classically or execute them on quantum hardware. We explain how measurement probabilities, expectation values, classical readouts, state overlaps, and circuit-level objectives support different transfer mechanisms. We then critically assess the presented results against baseline design, execution conditions, uncertainty, and resource costs. The evidence shows that distillation can improve selected quantum students, but benefits are task- and architecture-dependent, and several studies find negligible gains or even performance degradation. Evaluations on physical quantum hardware remain limited, with some testing individual quantum components rather than complete learning pipelines. Heterogeneous protocols and incomplete resource reporting prevent general conclusions about practical quantum advantage. We identify priorities for controlled benchmarking, noise-aware learning, reproducible evaluation, and efficient transfer between quantum and classical models.

A promising strategy uses quantum processors to prepare trial wavefunctions that guide classical quantum Monte Carlo (QMC). Here, we overcome this bottleneck by compressing quantum measurement data into machine-learning models that efficiently provide the wavefunction overlaps needed by QMC. This accelerates quantum-classical QMC by orders of magnitude, enabling chemically accurate simulations on up to 40 qubits, more than doubling the scale of previous hardware demonstrations with comparable accuracy. We demonstrate our approach on hydrogen chains and strongly correlated iron-sulfur clusters using measurements from Google's Willow processor. By enabling efficient reuse of quantum-state information on the classical coprocessor, our framework addresses a fundamental bottleneck in hybrid quantum algorithms that will persist even into the fault-tolerant era.

Bridging Pulse and Circuit Levels: A Geometric Framework for Deterministic Error Suppression

Junkai Zeng, Hao Liang, Yong-Ju Hai, Xiu-Hao Deng

Quantum errors in noisy environments remain a major obstacle to advancing quantum information technology. Standard quantum error correction requires massive ancillary qubit overhead, motivating the need for hardware-efficient mitigation strategies. In this work, we propose a framework for dynamical quantum error correction at the circuit level, requiring no logical encoding or ancillary qubits. By extending a geometric framework (originally developed for creating dynamical error-correcting gates at the control pulse level) to the discrete dynamics of digital circuits, we map the accumulation of coherent errors to trajectories in a high-dimensional error space. We demonstrate that inserting deterministically optimized twirling gate sequences actively shapes these trajectories, utilizing destructive interference to keep the accumulated error bounded with error scaling $\mathcal{O}(1)$. This deterministic path-shaping suppresses circuit errors fundamentally differently than the stochastic random-walk behavior of standard randomized compiling with error scaling $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{N})$. Furthermore, we show that this circuit-level dynamical correction synergizes with pulse-level robust control, providing an analytical bridge between continuous noise dynamics and discrete quantum compilation. This research illuminates pathways to achieving highly noise-resistant quantum circuits prior to the era of fault tolerance.

Ke-Xin Tang, Zhen-Qiang Weng, Kai Yan, Yao-Yao Lu, Yu-Ze Zhu, Hang Song

Designing protein sequences that satisfy both secondary structure requirements and solubility targets remains challenging. We present DiffSequence-Q, a hybrid multinomial diffusion model conditioned on secondary structure at each residue and a continuous solubility target. To introduce quantum auxiliary supervision during training, we employ a quantum neural network (QNN) or quantum Kolmogorov-Arnold network (QKAN) as an auxiliary solubility prediction head over shared Transformer representations. The auxiliary prediction loss is jointly optimized with the diffusion objective, allowing quantum auxiliary supervision to influence the learned denoising model. Once trained, protein sequences are generated entirely through classical reverse diffusion. Experimental characterization further demonstrated biological activity across three distinct protein families. Selected defensins exhibited pronounced inhibitory activity against Aspergillus niger, while xylanases and short-chain dehydrogenases exhibited substantial enzymatic activity. Together, these results support DiffSequence-Q as a framework for integrating structural requirements and solubility targets in protein generation, with experimentally demonstrated biological activity.

A Variational Quantum Algorithm for Multi-Skill Project Scheduling

Raul Martinez, Miguel Sánchez-Beato, Matilde Osa, Jorge Parra, Mario Calonge

Job scheduling is an NP-hard optimization problem with clear industrial relevance. In practice, distributing work across a team is not only about meeting deadlines or improving productivity; it also affects fatigue, stress, and employee well-being. Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs) provide a promising way to address these problems through QUBO formulations, although their scalability on near-term NISQ hardware is still limited by Hilbert-space growth and barren plateaus. In this work, we simulate a VQA for a realistic Multi-Skill Resource-Constrained Project Scheduling Problem (MS-RCPSP), including worker capacities, skill matrices, task priorities, deadlines, and calendar stability during rescheduling. To keep the problem within a near-term circuit size, we use a temporal batch decomposition that solves one day at a time, propagates the remaining workload, and combines it with a logarithmic binary encoding that keeps every batch at or below 30 qubits. Because the ground state of a QUBO Hamiltonian is a computational basis state, entanglement is not required to represent the solution, raising the question of whether it helps or hinders ansatz training. We address this question by comparing the linear and brick-wall entangling topologies of a hardware-efficient ansatz with an entanglement-free reference that has the same number of parameters and by training every configuration with COBYLA and with Adam using SPSA gradient estimation. For the instance and initialization studied, the entanglement-free reference produced feasible schedules under both optimizers. With COBYLA, it produced the feasible schedule closest to the required workload at a fraction of the simulation cost of the entangled topologies, indicating that entanglement did not improve the solutions found by the VQA.

Symmetry-guided algorithms for variational quantum optimization

Eunjin In, Kristina D. Launey, Darin C. Mumma, James A. Sauls

Quantum numbers associated with symmetries classify quantum states and identify the sectors relevant to a given simulation. Generic hardware-efficient ansatzes do not necessarily preserve the quantum numbers and may therefore explore states outside the target sector. We develop symmetry-guided algorithms based on the quantum-accessible Hilbert-Schmidt asymmetry $\mathrm{A}_\mathrm{HS}$, which quantifies the deviation of a quantum state from invariance under a specified symmetry group. This quantity guides the optimization toward a highly reduced symmetry-based target sector, without enforcing the symmetry on the preparation circuit. We demonstrate the approach as a proof of principle in variational quantum eigensolver calculations targeting singlet ground states with collective SU(2) symmetry. The applications include a five-qubit $S$-wave $p+{}^3\mathrm{He}$ two-cluster Hamiltonian, deduced from $\textit{ab initio}$ many-body calculations, and collective spin models with up to 12 particles, which provide a many-particle benchmark of asymmetry-guided optimization. Across these applications, $\mathrm{A}_\mathrm{HS}$-based guidance increases the singlet probability. Overall, the proposed algorithms improve energy accuracy during optimization and ground-state fidelity compared with direct energy minimization, with substantial fidelity improvements in the larger spin systems. These results demonstrate the utility of an asymmetry objective determined by the group action, independently of the Hamiltonian energy, for improving the reliability of variational quantum optimization.

Ultimately, this work delivers a practical transpilation method for DQC at scale.

Semi-Analytical Geometric Synthesis of Robust Quantum Controls

Eduardo Willwock Lussi, Rafael de Santiago, Eduardo Inacio Duzzioni, Hector Bessa Silveira

High-fidelity quantum gates are a central requirement for fault-tolerant quantum computation, yet their implementation is limited by the sensitivity of qubits to environmental noise and by control non-idealities. To mitigate these effects, quantum gates can be designed as dynamically corrected gates (DCGs), where control sequences are engineered to suppress the impact of errors on the resulting operation. Many methods in the literature are able to synthesize DCGs, but they typically rely on numerical optimization, with synthesis times on the order of minutes per gate. Here, we propose an automated semi-analytical method that reduces this cost by orders of magnitude, synthesizing smooth, robust control pulses in tens of milliseconds rather than minutes, with comparable fidelity and robustness. Our approach is based on the Space Curve Quantum Control (SCQC) formalism, where quantum evolution is mapped onto a geometric space curve whose geometric properties determine the target gate and its robustness. We parameterize the curve in the Frenet-Serret frame and employ the Pontryagin Maximum Principle (PMP) to design controls that are robust against both amplitude and dephasing errors. The PMP is formulated as a boundary-value problem and solved using a shooting method. The Frenet-Serret frame is integrated using Rodrigues' formula. Starting from a reference solution, we gradually vary the target rotation angle to construct a library of solutions, whose interpolation provides initial guesses for the shooting parameters of new target gates. We evaluated the method on 2,000 single-qubit rotations, obtaining a mean local optimization time of 39.945 ms per gate after preparation of the interpolation base. Quantum propagation yielded nominal gate infidelities below 10^-9 after virtual phase correction, with verified robustness against quasistatic amplitude and dephasing errors.

Hexagonal boron nitride (hBN) flakes have been extensively studied as a host material for SPEs, though working in two dimensions is accompanied by engineering complexity. Improved Exact and Approximate Quantum Fourier Transforms from Multiplication

Chandrima Kayal, Rajat Mittal, Manaswi Paraashar, Nitin Saurabh

In the world of quantum computing, quantum Fourier transform (QFT) has been one of the most important subroutines for quantum algorithms, as exemplified by Shor's factoring algorithms and phase estimation (which in itself has been used as a primitive in various quantum algorithms). In fact most of the well known quantum algorithms can be divided into two classes, QFT based algorithms and search based algorithms. The usual circuit for QFT (covered in almost every basic quantum algorithms course) needs $O(q^2)$ gates (and $O(q)$ qubits) to perform the exact Fourier transform over $\mathbb{F}_{2^q}$. Cleve and Watrous (FOCS 2000) improved this gate count to $O(q (\log q)^2 (\log \log q))$ using the integer multiplication algorithm in $O(n (\log n) (\log \log n))$ time for two $n$-bit integers (best known at that time). Our first main result shows that this gate count can be improved to $O(q (\log q))$. In fact, if there is a multiplication circuit, to multiply two $n$-bit integers, with $O(n (\log n)^α)$ gates; we get a QFT circuit with $O(q (\log q)^α)$ gates (improving upon $O(q (\log q)^{α+ 1})$) gates from the construction of Cleve and Watrous). Given the recent claim by OpenAI about integer multiplication, this opens up the possibility of having a QFT circuit with $o \left( q (\log q)\right)$ gates. On approximating the QFT over $\ZZ/(2^q \ZZ)$, the result of Ahokas, Cleve and Hales (ERATO 2003) showed that an approximating circuit can be built with $O \left( q (\log \log(q/\varepsilon))^{α+1} \right)$ gates, where $ε$ is the approximating parameter. Our second main result again improves this to $O \left( q (\log \log(q/\varepsilon))^α \right)$ gates.
