Weight-Space Programming of Variational Quantum Circuits: Large Language Models Write Quantum Circuit Parameters Without an Optimizer

Debarshi Kundu, Swaroop Ghosh, Vasant G Honavar

Variational quantum algorithms (VQAs) optimize circuit angles through repeated quantum-device evaluations, often facing vanishing gradients and high optimization costs. We ask whether a large language model (LLM) can write these angles directly. Extending *weight-space programming* from classical networks to quantum circuits, we provide a frontier LLM (\gpt) with the problem, circuit, and gate conventions. Its angles are executed exactly as written, without returning losses, gradients, or accuracy feedback. Across 139 requests, we test handwritten-digit classification, VQE ground-state estimation for spin chains and H$_2$, and MaxCut QAOA, comparing against random weights, classical optimization, and optimization initialized with LLM weights on the same circuit. When parameters have interpretable structure, the LLM matches or approaches the optimizer: it achieves 95--100\% of the energy spectrum on every VQE problem, derives the exact H$_2$ ground state in closed form, finds optimal depth-one QAOA angles for every graph, and reaches 93--96\% accuracy on digits 3 versus 8 with 12--18 hand-written weights, outperforming an optimizer trained on the same ten examples. Its weights are also strong warm starts: optimization reaches its best value after a median of one evaluation rather than hundreds. Allowed to design circuits, the LLM chooses data-dependent rotation chains encoding pixel importance in all 30 cases (one qubit for binary tasks, one chain per qubit for ten classes), reaching 65\% accuracy across ten digits. Yet the approach fails in opaque weight spaces: accuracy drops to chance when fixed circuits exceed 30 weights, while optimization improves. It is also sensitive to gate conventions: 6 of 42 QAOA replies reverse mixing-angle signs and fail. We release all prompts, replies, and scores.

Realizing Unstructured PDE Forecasting on Superconducting Quantum Processors

Erik Connerty, Jonathan Eastridge, Taylor Patti, Gerasimos Angelatos, Ethan N. Evans

Simulating high dimensional unstructured Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) with quantum processors is a major challenge with significant potential benefit. Existing methods have been demonstrated at small qubit counts, on simplifications of realistic problems. We implement a quantum reservoir network (QRN) for forecasting high-dimensional unstructured PDEs using up to 52 qubits on IBM superconducting hardware. Our method is gradient-free and does not suffer from the challenges of variational quantum algorithms. Instead, the QRN provides a large kernel space, up to 2^52 dimensions and beyond the reach of exact classical simulation, that remains useful under current hardware noise levels. Furthermore, a partial measure-and-reset mechanism enables total circuit runtime for ~24 times the qubit coherence time, so qubit lifetimes do not limit the forecast horizon. Thus, our method enables forecasting realistic unstructured nonlinear PDEs on quantum hardware. Typical difficulties associated with predicting large scale PDEs, such as transpilation, exponential shot requirements, hardware noise, and short qubit lifetimes are mitigated through careful design considerations and a dynamic observable reconstruction method. We validate our approach using a ~34k cell unstructured 2D cylinder flow at Reynolds number 1000 and a challenging nonlinear Gray--Scott reaction--diffusion problem, demonstrating high fidelity prediction on CUDA-Q simulation and on 26 and 52-qubit superconducting quantum processors. Specifically, we show that adding QRN features to an autoencoder linear prediction pipeline reduces prediction error by 21%-43%. Finally, we demonstrate closed-loop autoregressive PDE forecasting on these problems. This work demonstrates, for the first time, quantum circuits with depth beyond qubit lifetimes achieving accurate unstructured mesh forecasting on highly nonlinear PDEs.

Towards large-scale ab initio Molecular Dynamics Simulation with Efficient Sampling-based Quantum Computing Algorithms

Ankit Khandelwal, Nirmal M R, Aaryav Mishra, Debasmita Bhoumik, Dhinakaran Vinayagamurthy, M Girish Chandra

Ab initio molecular dynamics (AIMD) requires the calculation of highly accurate electronic-structure energies and nuclear forces at every time step, which can quickly become computationally expensive. Sample-based quantum diagonalization offers a route to such calculations on quantum hardware, but its use in AIMD simulations has so far been limited to active spaces of about 20 qubits. In these spaces, exact diagonalization is tractable, and the quantum circuits are typically built from standard coupled-cluster amplitudes. Here, we introduce SqDRIFT-AIMD, which combines randomized sample-based Krylov quantum diagonalization (SqDRIFT) with analytical nuclear gradients. With SqDRIFT-AIMD, we obtain more accurate ground-state energies than the conventional coupled-cluster singles and doubles (CCSD) driven AIMD simulation for the $40$-qubit active space. We apply SqDRIFT-AIMD to study the dynamics of proton transfer in the Zundel cation, using circuits executed on IBM Heron r3 quantum processors. SqDRIFT energies fall below CCSD across the $40$-qubit double-well PES and stay within a few m$E_\text{h}$ of CCSD at 52 qubits. The $40$-qubit SqDRIFT-AIMD trajectory tracks the SHCI-driven proton transfer to within $1.33$ mÅ and exhibits excellent total energy conservation, with a standard deviation of only $0.245$ m$E_\text{h}$. These results extend quantum-centric AIMD to active spaces beyond the reach of exact diagonalization.

Sign-hedged Beam QAOA:Scaling Quantum Optimization Algorithms

Anupama Ray, Arush Sharma

Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) encodes a combinatorial objective as an Ising Hamiltonian and alternates cost and mixer operations for p rounds. Recursive QAOA (RQAOA) scales further by using the resulting correlations, whether variables tend to agree or disagree, to eliminate variables one at a time. But each inferred sign is accepted irrevocably, so a single early mistake propagates through every later reduction and can discard the global optimum. As elimination proceeds, residuals shrink while effective edge density typically rises, so later decisions face denser Ising problems, a regime that fully connected Sherrington Kirkpatrick (SK) instances stress test directly. This paper proposes sign-hedged beam RQAOA, which retains the variable or pair selected by standard RQAOA, explores both possible sign assignments, keeps the best B residual problems according to a classical score, and completes each survivor with ordinary RQAOA. On frustrated SK instances, where standard recursion collapses to AR 0.474, beam recovers near-optimal AR 0.998 at the same p. We evaluate the workflow on 50+ instances from HamLib and QOBLIB, spanning 4 and 5 regular and Erdos Renyi MaxCut graphs, Sherrington Kirkpatrick (SK) spin glasses, and cardinality-constrained portfolio optimization, with up to 60 qubits and hardware runs on ibm kingston and ibm marrakesh. Matched simulator and device cells show consistent beam rankings: simulation and hardware agree that beam recovers near-optimal approximation ratio (AR) where standard recursion fails. These gains come from additional residual-circuit evaluations, classical pruning, and exact solution of a small core, rather than from increasing p, beam is a decision-side optimization atop fixed-p QAOA/RQAOA, not a claim that quantum circuits alone scale with qubit count.

Sequential vs parallel Strategies for Sharing Quantum Advantage in Communication Tasks

Satyaki Manna, Arindam Mitra, Sumit Mukherjee

Information can be extracted from a quantum system in a sustainable manner, allowing the system to be reused for further information extraction through subsequent interactions. We investigate the possibility of consensus between two experimentally feasible methods, namely, parallel and sequential sharing, that enable multiple observers to extract information encoded in a quantum system. We show that any statistics generated by a parallel sharing strategy can be reproduced by a sequential strategy when only two observers are involved. However, when more than two observers participate in sharing quantum correlations, certain statistics achievable through parallel strategies cannot be reproduced by sequential strategies. Moreover, we demonstrate that sharing quantum advantage through a parallel strategy is impossible for a class of communication tasks involving two decoding measurements, thereby establishing an arbitrarily large gap between the sustainability of quantum advantage under sequential and parallel strategies. This naturally raises the question of whether this impossibility extends to communication tasks involving more than two decoding measurements. We show that, while parallel sharing of quantum advantage is impossible in a 3-bit random access code (RAC), it becomes possible in a 3-bit parity-oblivious RAC, where at most two observers can simultaneously obtain a quantum advantage by performing measurements on two imperfect clones of the initially prepared quantum system. Our findings have potential applications in the development of quantum communication networks and distributed quantum information processing.

Rotational Encoding of Objective Functions for Quantum Optimization

Sonia Yang, Ali Al-Bayaty, Shanyan Chen, Marek Perkowski

In this paper, we introduce the Angular Rotational Counter (ARC), a novel approach to represent objective functions that can be decomposed as a weighted sum of Boolean functions. ARC encodes the objective as an accumulated sum of quantum rotations onto a single-qubit register instead of a multi-qubit register used in much of the previous literature. We mathematically establish and experimentally validate that these rotations sum to the objective value for all possible solutions and derive the relationship between the resulting measurement probabilities and the objective. This design was applied to construct a quantum oracle for the state assignment problem of finite automata and state machines using a weighted Hamming distance cost function, which is a special case of the quadratic assignment problem. We evaluate this oracle design on 26 benchmarks and compare it with corresponding Boolean-based oracles on four cost metrics. Across these benchmarks, the oracle with ARC produces a mean reduction in the qubit count of 20.8%, gate count of 89.3%, circuit depth of 92.4%, and quantum cost of 90.6%. These results demonstrate substantial reductions in circuit cost for the evaluated benchmarks and establish rotational encoding as a promising approach for representing objective functions in optimization based on quantum search algorithms.

Delta-Corrected Shot Reduction for Bypassing Optimization Plateaus in Variational Quantum Eigensolvers

Jamie Evander Ghozaly, Pekik Nurwantoro, Gagus Ketut Sunnardianto, Yanoar P. Sarwono, Leong-Chuan Kwek

The variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) remains a cornerstone hybrid quantum-classical algorithm for quantum simulation, yet its scalability is fundamentally constrained by heavy measurement overheads. While strategies such as variance-preserved shot reduction (VPSR) mitigates this cost, they suffer from convergence instability and optimization stagnation caused by finite-sample bias in limited pilot shots. To overcome this limitation, we introduce delta-corrected shot reduction (DCSR), an analytically enhanced shot allocation strategy that rigorously mitigates finite-sample instability. Utilizing the multivariate delta method, we derive explicit higher-order bias correction terms for the empirical reduction factor. Both theoretical analysis and numerical simulations reveal that incorporating higher-order statistical moments, such as skewness and kurtosis, provides the gradient resolution necessary to bypass noise-induced barren plateaus. DSCR significantly accelerates convergence compared to standard VPSR across diverse noise and resource regimes while matching or decreasing cumulative measurement costs, establishing a mathematically rigorous framework for variance-stabilized shot allocation on near-term hardware.
