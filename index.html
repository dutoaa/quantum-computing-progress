Cqlib: A High-Performance Software Stack for Quantum Computing

Tianyan Quantum Group

This paper presents Cqlib, a high-performance software stack for quantum computing built around a unified quantum circuit model. Its native Rust core provides efficient implementations of circuit processing, quantum-state and operator computation, compilation, and optimization, while Python and C interfaces support algorithm development and system integration. Cqlib covers quantum program construction, simulation, analysis, compilation, device adaptation, and result processing under consistent program semantics. Through OpenQASM and the Quantum Control Instruction Set (QCIS), Cqlib supports program representation and hardware-oriented execution. Cqlib further provides backend interfaces for device execution, with Cqlib-Tianyan serving as one implementation for QCIS-compatible quantum hardware. Evaluations using parameterized compilation tasks from Benchpress and related tests show that Cqlib achieves good functional coverage, compilation correctness, and execution efficiency within the evaluated task scope. We develop Cqlib-Algorithm as a modular library of reusable operators and composable components for widely used quantum algorithms, including quantum search, phase estimation, and variational algorithms, with additional support for error mitigation. To complement these building blocks with domain-specific workflows, we further develop Cqlib-VQE, Cqlib-QAOA, and Cqlib-QML for application-level studies in quantum chemistry, combinatorial optimization, and quantum machine learning, respectively. Cqlib also integrates source-grounded Skills and MCP-based tools to support agent-assisted program generation, backend execution, and result-guided workflow refinement. Overall, Cqlib provides a unified and extensible software foundation that connects quantum algorithm development, simulation, compilation, and execution on QCIS-compatible quantum hardware.

Our work not only proposes a quantum large model that can significantly reduce the computational cost, but also promotes the quantum computation towards general computing.

Observable-Level Expressivity in Fixed-Readout Quantum Neural Networks

Iqra Bashir, Yuanfeng Wang, Mingda Huang, Keren Li, Qi Qin

A central question in quantum machine learning is which functions of an input state a model can represent when its measurement is fixed by hardware or by experimental design. We address this question for quantum neural networks with a fixed readout observable, where the trainable pre-measurement dynamics determine the effective observable in the Heisenberg picture and the scalar output is the measured expectation value itself, without trainable affine post-processing. We characterize the reachable effective observables under three physical resource classes: unitary evolution, which preserves the spectrum of the readout; mixed-unitary evolution, which relaxes this to a majorization constraint while preserving the trace; and completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) evolution, whose Heisenberg adjoint is unital rather than trace-preserving and therefore lifts the trace constraint inherited at the mixed-unitary level. The three reachable sets are a fixed-spectrum orbit, its convex hull, and an operator interval, and the inclusions are strict for any readout that is not proportional to the identity. The separation is witnessed locally: for a fixed Pauli readout $Z_j$ on an arbitrary number of qubits, the amplitude-damping observable $γ\id+(1-γ)Z_j$ lies outside the mixed-unitary level for every $γ>0$. We realize the CPTP level with a Stinespring-channelized circuit and derive closed-form loss floors, $4γ^2/3$ and $γ^2$ for the unitary and mixed-unitary levels and zero at the CPTP level, which the numerics reproduce within precision. Structured-observable, Rényi-moment and classification experiments illustrate how the hierarchy manifests in finite-depth ansätze. The results identify which spectral constraint each physical resource relaxes, framing reachable-observable classes as a resource for expressivity rather than an architectural advantage.

Binary-Tree Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm for the Maximum $k$-Cut Problem

Zhaoyang Song, Wei Zhang, Ji Cao, Xuexi Yi, Hong-Fu Wang

The maximum $k$-cut (Max-$k$-Cut) problem, as a natural generalization of the maximum-cut (MaxCut) problem, has broader applications in practical engineering. To solve this problem on quantum devices, existing studies typically encode the $k$ possible subset assignments of each vertex within the quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA), which directly causes the required quantum resources to grow substantially with $k$. Here we propose a binary-tree quantum approximate optimization algorithm (BT-QAOA), which solves the Max-$k$-Cut problem by applying standard MaxCut QAOA to at most $2k-3$ subproblems. Specifically, after solving MaxCut once on the full vertex set, each subsequent step splits only the subset that yields the largest cut-value gain. This process is equivalent to constructing a binary tree whose leaf nodes after $k-1$ steps form an approximate solution to the Max-$k$-Cut problem. Furthermore, we derive a theoretical lower bound on the approximation ratio in terms of the single-step splitting quality, showing that the overall approximation ratio does not decay exponentially across multiple splits. Numerical simulations show that BT-QAOA substantially reduces the single-circuit qubit and gate overhead while maintaining a high approximation ratio, improving the estimated success probability of a single compiled circuit by orders of magnitude. This work effectively expands the practical reach of QAOA on near-term quantum hardware, offering a new route for solving more complex real-world optimization problems with limited quantum resources.

Jack Mandell, Adam Leicester, Zixu Wang, Jian Shi, Haimeng Zhang, Yangyang Xu

Structure-aware optimizers have been the focus of recent study for the optimization of parameters in variational quantum circuits. Many of these optimizers follow a coordinate-based paradigm, making single parameter updates by using classical approximations to the cost function generated from quantum sampling. However, the performance of structure-aware optimizers on real quantum hardware at large system size has yet to be demonstrated. Recently, Interpolation-based Coordinate Descent (ICD) has been proposed to unify many of these optimizers into one framework. ICD requires a preset frequency set for interpolation, which can be theoretically derived, but for which the cost function is generally sparse over and may require manual tuning. To address these issues, we propose Frequency-learning Interpolation-based Coordinate Descent (FLICD) to adaptively learn frequencies online, allowing easier scaling up to larger systems. We demonstrate that our algorithm is more accurate than an existing zeroth-order method COBYLA and requires fewer quantum resources than ICD on simulator. Using IBM quantum hardware, we execute FLICD on systems up to 100 qubits and observe robust performance with an increasing number of layers.

Eitaro Ishikawa, Noah Hirai, Koichiro Miyanishi, Hana Ebi, Kazufumi Tanji, Isamu Kudo

The quantum charge-coupled device (QCCD) architecture is a promising approach to scalable trapped-ion quantum computing, enabling flexible qubit interactions by transporting ions between dedicated trap zones. Programs for such systems require operations beyond conventional circuit primitives: ion shuttling, physical SWAPs, trap splitting and merging, and cooling. Motional excitation accumulated during transport increases the mean phonon number of ions and degrades the fidelity of the two-qubit Molmer-Sorensen gates. However, existing quantum error correction (QEC) simulation frameworks do not jointly track per-ion motional excitation and explicitly schedule cooling operations with their transport time and heating costs. We develop a full-stack software framework for QEC experiments and simulations on linear QCCD systems with a single ion species, comprising an intermediate representation (IR) supporting QCCD-native operations, a compiler that translates syndrome-extraction circuits into the IR, and a heating-aware noise-injection pipeline that generates noisy Stim circuits. The compiler updates the mean phonon number of each ion throughout the schedule, and the pipeline derives gate-dependent error rates from the tracked phonon states and per-ion timing, enabling the evaluation of finite-size logical-error scaling of the rotated surface code under transport- and heating-aware noise. We show that transport-induced heating prevents logical error rates from decreasing with increasing code distance under the evaluated conditions, and that indirectly cooling the ion pairs involved in two-qubit gates, by merging them with precooled coolant ions immediately before each gate, restores this decrease. By sweeping the duration and post-cooling mean phonon number of an abstract cooling instruction, we characterize their trade-off and identify the region in which logical-error scaling is maintained.

Modular exponentiation dominates the logical cost of factoring 2048-bit RSA integers (RSA-2048) with Shor's algorithm. Recent approaches based on approximate residue number systems (RNS) sharply reduce the required logical qubits, yet still demand billions of Toffoli gates. Optimizing these circuits is difficult because shared ancilla qubits and measurement-based uncomputation tightly couple the arithmetic stages, while rare overflows in compressed registers occur too infrequently for random simulation to detect. Here we use large language model (LLM)-driven search to redesign the RNS pipeline across stages and build a deterministic compiler that produces a Pareto frontier of RSA-2048 circuits. Relative to Gidney's 2025 baseline under matched adder accounting, our frontier cuts the minimum qubit-Toffoli product by 70.1% and lowers expected factoring Toffolis by up to 78.6% at matched accumulator precision, bringing RSA-2048 factoring below one billion Toffolis for the first time.

Impact of structured noise on bosonic quantum error correction

Guoyu Shu, Ling Hu, Hongbo Wu, Zhongchu Ni, Yanyan Cai, Xiaowei Deng

Bosonic quantum error correction relies on an accurate description of single-photon loss dissipation using an ideal annihilation operator, under which correctable loss errors act equivalently on different logical code states. In realistic weakly nonlinear bosonic modes, however, coupling to a structured environment can render photon loss Fock-state dependent, thereby modifying the effective dissipation operator and deforming the associated error space. Here, we experimentally investigate this effect in a three-dimensional circuit quantum electrodynamics system. By injecting spectrally engineered environmental noise into a weakly nonlinear oscillator, we observe pronounced frequency selectivity in the measured Fock-state dynamics. While the long-time transition rates converge to the Markovian limit, the transient dynamics display distinctly non-Markovian behavior, in quantitative agreement with a second-order time-convolutionless theory. By applying narrowband noise to a binomially encoded logical qubit, we further demonstrate Fock-state-dependent dissipation channels that drive logical states beyond the nominally correctable error space. These results establish structured environmental noise as a mechanism for deforming photon-loss errors in weakly nonlinear bosonic systems and highlight the need to incorporate realistic, state-dependent dissipation into the design and characterization of bosonic quantum error correction.

Quantum simulation of low-temperature impurity models

Jiaqing Jiang, Nathan Ju

Quantum impurity models, which describe a small but strongly-interacting subsystem coupled to a large noninteracting bath, are the computational core of dynamical mean field theory and play an important role in simulating strongly correlated materials. We develop quantum algorithms for efficiently simulating these models at low temperatures and with high precision. For any impurity model on $n$ fermionic modes with fixed impurity size: (1) We give a quantum algorithm that prepares the thermal state at inverse temperature $β$ to precision $δ$ in time $\mathrm{poly}(n,β,\log(1/δ))$. This algorithm is the first to achieve both polynomial scaling in the inverse temperature and polylogarithmic scaling in the precision. When the Hamiltonian has an inverse-polynomial spectral gap, our quantum algorithm prepares its ground state to precision $δ$ in time $\mathrm{poly}(n,\log(1/δ))$. (2) We further give a quantum algorithm that prepares a purification of the thermal state in time $\mathrm{poly}(n,β,\log(1/δ))$. Using this purification, we estimate static and dynamical properties with error $δ$ in time polynomial in $n$ and $β$ and linear in $1/δ$ up to polylogarithmic factors, achieving the Heisenberg scaling. Our algorithms build on quantum Gibbs samplers, which are quantum analogues of Markov chain Monte Carlo methods. By using global jump operators instead of local ones, we avoid the $\exp(β)$ overhead commonly associated with quantum Gibbs samplers, making them efficient for impurity models even at low temperatures.

Stopping-Time Leakage in Blind Quantum Delegation

Noureldin Mohamed, Saif Al-Kuwari

Blind quantum computation lets a client delegate a computation while hiding it from the server, up to a declared leakage. Its security definitions cover a single round. A variational quantum algorithm, however, delegates many rounds, and its classical optimizer decides from private data when to stop. We study what the resulting round count tells an honest-but-curious server. For protocols with one-time-padded transcripts and fresh randomness in every round, we prove that the server's best distinguishing advantage over the whole sequence equals the total-variation distance between the stopping-time distributions, so the round count is the only residual channel in this model. We also show that soundness composes as $δ\,\mathbb{E}[T]$ under adaptive stopping, given a conditional per-round soundness property that composably secure verifiable protocols provide. In simulated delegated eigensolvers for the transverse-field Ising chain, the local Polyak-Lojasiewicz ratio measured in iterations 5 to 15 predicts the noiseless stopping round with a leave-one-out error of 1.7 iterations, compared with 10.3 for a constant predictor. Across 14 configurations that differ in optimizer, stopping rule, initialization, system size, shot budget and problem family, the leakage is significant in 12 and ranges from 0.14 to 1.68 bits, and a maximum-entropy bound estimated on held-out sessions lies above it in all of them. For defense, we show that any mechanism acting after the stopping decision can only add rounds, and that differentially private padding costs the full adjacency radius. A fixed public iteration budget removes the channel completely; set at the median run length, it costs 10% of the remaining energy gap. A differentially private stopping rule based on AboveThreshold protects every pair of instances, but it must often stop too early, which makes it costly.

Local complementation, which toggles adjacencies among the neighbors of a selected vertex, produces operationally equivalent graph states up to an inexpensive operation called local Clifford gates. In this paper, we formulate the problem of finding a desirable equivalent graph via local complementations as a state space search problem. Our experimental evaluation shows that despite its simplicity, GBFS outperforms existing approaches based on integer linear programming or local search across multiple graph classes, including random graphs, bounded-degree graphs, and most of the graphs constructed from quantum LDPC codes. In addition, we empirically analyze GBFS's search behavior, confirming that we need search rather than stopping at the first local minimum to find the best solution.

Froq: Safe Speculative Execution on Live Quantum States for FTQC

Yicheng Guang, Yueqi Chen

Reducing execution time is critical to fault-tolerant quantum computing (FTQC) as longer executions expose logical qubits to more opportunities for failure. However, quantum processors could frequently stall due to slow classical error-correction decoders. Recent speculative decoding accelerates this process, but its speculation remains on the classical side, leaving quantum operations waiting for confirmed decoding results. Extending speculative execution to the quantum side is fundamentally challenging. Unlike classical processor states, quantum states cannot be arbitrarily copied or checkpointed for rollback, making recovery from mispredictions difficult. In this work, we propose Froq to enable safe speculative execution on live quantum states. Froq uses compiler-assisted recovery contracts to track how mispredictions propagate through quantum operations. It further employs a resource-aware runtime mechanism to reserve recovery workspace and dynamically balance the benefits of speculative execution against the expected costs of recovery. We evaluate Froq on eight FTQC programs across 88 configurations, building on an existing speculative window decoder. Froq reduces quantum-side time by 9.81% across all 88 configurations. The reduction reaches 18.34% across the 45 configurations allowing concurrent predictions, and peaks at 31.1% at code distance d=21, under limited resources.
