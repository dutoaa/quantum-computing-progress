Resource Analysis of Quantum Optimization Algorithms via Variational Methods

Vitor Dallabona, Lucas Friedrich, Jonas Maziero

Variational Quantum Algorithms (VQAs) are promising approaches for solving combinatorial optimization problems on near-term quantum devices. However, the role played by quantum resources such as entanglement and coherence during the optimization process remains poorly understood. In this work, we investigate the evolution of these resources in two VQAs--the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) and the Feedback-based Algorithm for Quantum Optimization (FALQON)--applied to the Minimum Vertex Cover problem. We employ a variational resource-quantification framework based on the Bures metric and Uhlmann's theorem, in which an auxiliary quantum circuit is optimized to identify the closest resource-free states. This approach allows us to quantify Bures entanglement and Bures coherence of the algorithmic states without requiring full quantum state tomography. For the considered problem instance, QAOA exhibits a pronounced accumulation of entanglement in its initial layers, followed by a progressive reduction as the algorithm converges toward the optimal solution. Its coherence likewise decreases overall with increasing circuit depth, although a finite residual value persists due to the degeneracy of the optimal-solution subspace. In contrast, FALQON exhibits a substantially lower entanglement peak, reached at larger circuit depth, followed by a decay as convergence proceeds. Its coherence decreases smoothly and monotonically together with the optimization error. These results reveal distinct quantum-resource trajectories associated with the two optimization strategies and illustrate how entanglement and coherence evolve as variational quantum states approach solutions of a classical combinatorial optimization problem.

Automating quantum circuit optimization with AI agents

Marcos Crichigno, Steve Flammia

Compiling quantum algorithms for real devices involves hard optimization problems that can decide whether an algorithm runs at scale. We present a general method for solving such problems with AI agents, with every result certified. It builds on autoresearch loops, in which agents iteratively improve programs against a fixed evaluator and which have recently improved quantum cryptanalysis circuits, and adds two elements: a frozen, exact verifier certifies every result, and this verifier, together with the challenge around it, is built by an agent that formulates the problem with a researcher and validates the verifier against a solved case, deliberately broken solutions and published results before any solution exists. The method applies to any optimization problem whose solutions can be verified exactly and efficiently. We apply it to three compilation problems in quantum simulation, namely fermion-to-qubit encoding, Hamiltonian layout and unitary synthesis. In each, the agents improved substantially on published or standard constructions, reducing the cost being optimized by up to 73% at the largest sizes tested and outperforming a state-of-the-art optimizer for fermion-to-qubit encodings. Every submission is a program accompanied by its agent's notes, so the ideas behind each improvement are recorded in human-readable form; later agents built on them, and we describe them here. These results open the way to automated, certified discovery across quantum circuit design and beyond.

Quantum Fokker-Planck solvers as trainable generative models

Tariq Mahassen, Marcin Jastrzebski, Oleksandr Kyriienko

We develop and analyze generative models based on quantum solvers of stochastic differential equations. Unlike the common approach based on layered variational quantum circuits with parameterized rotations, we parameterize the potential of a Langevin process. The associated Fokker-Planck (FP) equations are Hermitianized and solved using a linear combination of cosine operators implemented through quantum singular value transformation. By encoding the square root of the stationary density, the model enables sampling through computational-basis measurements and is efficiently controlled by coefficients of the block-encoded diagonal potential. We train the proposed model to match empirical distributions within the family described by FP equations, demonstrating parameter recovery for multivariate probability distributions. Crucially, the model remains trainable at scale. Sample-efficient and convex optimization for in-distribution targets arises from a physics-informed inductive bias and holds regardless of the dimensionality of the problem. We provide circuit constructions and efficient block encodings of the main components of the algorithm and analyze resource requirements. Our approach frames quantum generative learning as quantum model discovery and provides a foundation for extending this framework to high-dimensional, strongly correlated scientific data.

Mamba Quantum Eigensolver (MQE): A Selective State Space Model for Scalable Ground State Simulations in Quantum Chemistry

G. X. A. Petronilo, M. R. Araújo, Erico S. Teixeira, Clebson Cruz

Predicting molecular electronic structure using Quantum Computing approaches is a challenge in Computational Chemistry. Traditional Variational Quantum Eigensolvers (VQEs) are typically hindered by optimization challenges. The alternative Generative Quantum Eigensolver (GQE) mitigates this by reformulating ground-state search as discrete sequence generation, but its Transformer backbone suffers from quadratic computational complexity, bottlenecking scalability for larger molecules. To address this, we introduce the Mamba Quantum Eigensolver (Mamba-QE), which replaces the Transformer with a Selective State Space Model (SSM) to process operator sequences with linear $\mathcal{O}(L)$ complexity. Additionally, we integrate a distance-conditioned mechanism enabling the direct prediction of complete potential energy surfaces (PES). Evaluated on the $H_2$ molecule, Mamba-QE reproduces Full Configuration Interaction (FCI) reference energies well within chemical accuracy, achieving an energy error below $3\times10^{-6}$ Ha across 16 geometries with zero variational violations. Mamba-QE maintains high expressive power while establishing a robust, scalable foundation for quantum simulations of complex molecular systems.

Digital Quantum Simulation of Dynamics in Interacting Fields

Zhiyao Li

Matter under extreme conditions behaves as a non-equilibrium, strongly-interacting quantum system. Such systems are in the non-perturbative regime of quantum chromodynamics (QCD), where their time evolution is classically intractable. Quantum computing offers a promising alternative, but present-day devices are limited in qubit count and circuit depth, so efficient formulations and algorithms are key to scaling toward experimentally relevant QCD simulations. A scalable framework is established for simulating energy loss and hadronization in 1+1-dimensional SU(2) lattice gauge theory, in which a heavy quark traverses a lattice of light quarks. Classical lattice QCD methods are translated into physics-informed circuits to prepare the interacting ground state. The evolution is run on IBM's quantum processor ibm_pittsburgh, where the charge distribution and energy loss are measured. A further study develops the quantum simulation of the fully gauge-fixed SU(2) Hamiltonian in a mixed basis, a formulation that is expected to remain efficient at all gauge couplings. Digitizing the continuous gauge fields with as few as three qubits per plaquette reproduces low-energy observables with $10^{-3}$ precision. Scaling to larger systems is limited by resource overhead, which motivates truncations that preserve the relevant observables. Sequency Hierarchy Truncation (SeqHT) exploits the hierarchy in basis-operator contributions. Demonstrated on the adiabatic preparation of the $λφ^4$ ground state on ibm_sherbrooke, SeqHT reduces circuit depth by ~30%, suppressing hardware noise in the extracted observables. All simulations in this dissertation are validated on quantum hardware against classical benchmarks. These studies advance quantum simulations of real-time QCD dynamics.

Quantum Broker: QoS-Aware Distribution of Quantum Computations

Giuseppe Bisicchia, Alessandro Bocci, Jose Garcia-Alonso, Juan Manuel Murillo, Antonio Brogi

Cloud-accessible quantum computing has made heterogeneous Quantum Processing Units (QPUs) available through provider platforms that differ in qubit technology, calibration state, queueing behavior, and pricing model. This heterogeneity makes execution management a quantum-engineering concern: a quantum circuit may be executable on several backends, but the resulting cost, latency, fidelity, and reproducibility depend on backend selection and on how measurement shots are allocated. This paper presents the Quantum Broker, an open-source and provider-agnostic framework for Quality-of-Service-aware quantum execution management. The framework operationalizes shot-wise distribution by exploiting the statistical independence of quantum measurement shots to dispatch a single circuit across multiple heterogeneous QPUs according to user-defined constraints and optimization objectives. It combines a modular execution architecture, interchangeable backend-profiling strategies, and the Quantum QoS Specification Language (QQSpec), a declarative language for expressing QoS requirements. Dispatch planning is supported by two solver backends: a mixed-integer linear-programming (MILP) solver, and a nonlinear dual-annealing solver. Experiments on benchmark quantum circuits and noisy simulator backends show that the Quantum Broker can produce feasible QoS-aware dispatch plans under heterogeneous backend conditions and can improve QoS satisfaction with respect to single-backend and naive multi-backend baselines. The results also expose a practical trade-off between planning time and dispatch quality, with the nonlinear solver producing higher-quality allocations at increased computational cost. Overall, the Quantum Broker provides an extensible execution-management layer for engineering portable, reproducible, and QoS-aware quantum software over fragmented quantum cloud infrastructures.

Vivan Bhatt, Zhongtian Dong, Kyoungchul Kong, Kwangmin Yu

We study variational preparation of ground states and sector-resolved excited states in the $(1+1)$-dimensional lattice Gross-Neveu model. We construct fixed circuits preserving either total fermion number or every flavor number, and a problem-specific scalable-circuit adaptive variational quantum eigensolver (SC-ADAPT-VQE). Classical circuit benchmarks cover massless and massive systems through 30 qubits, using exact-diagonalization and tensor-network reference results. Imposing flavor-number conservation improves the achieved energies in the completed two-flavor comparisons. Selected massive fixed-ansatz benchmarks with one to four flavors have relative energy errors of approximately $1 \times 10^{-5}$ - $2 \times 10^{-4}$, smaller than the quoted literature errors where available, although discretizations and computational resources differ. At fixed flavor number, range caps, and append count, the adaptive construction has a precompilation local-factor count linear in volume and a volume-independent shared-parameter count; fixed-accuracy performance at arbitrary volumes remains unestablished. High-fidelity and robust controlled-Z gates for superconducting transmon qubits. I. Intrinsic errors and decoherence

Chia-Hsien Huang, Hsi-Sheng Goan

Fault-tolerant quantum computing requires quantum gates that combine short duration, high fidelity, and robustness to noise. As quantum processors scale, robustness becomes increasingly important because exhaustive characterization and frequent recalibration of individual gates become impractical. We present a comprehensive optimal-control framework for controlled-Z (CZ) gates between directly coupled, frequency-tunable transmons that systematically accounts for intrinsic Hamiltonian errors and realistic decoherence mechanisms, including energy relaxation and dephasing. Using smooth, single-shot flux pulses, we obtain 12-ns CZ gates---close to the theoretical speed limit of 11.6 ns---with intrinsic errors suppressed to the $2\times10^{-5}$ level. We construct dephasing noise models, calibrated against published experimental measurements, that capture fast dephasing from room-temperature control electronics and slow dephasing from $1/f$ flux noise. Using these models, we identify dephasing sweet spots that suppress sensitivity to dephasing noise. We further exploit transmon asymmetry as a design parameter to access distinct noise-resilient operating regimes. These results establish an experimentally applicable framework for balancing gate speed, fidelity, and noise robustness while reducing characterization and recalibration overhead.

The codes are obtained from the flexible concatenation of small-distance high-rate self-dual CSS codes. Addressable and parallel universal logic on the resulting $K$ logical qubits is injected by fault-tolerantly preparing and then consuming diverse logical $K$-qubit CSS stabilizer states and logical $\lvert H \rangle^{\otimes K}$ global magic states. Flag-at-origin is used to prepare the stabilizer resource states while a concatenated cultivation protocol prepares the global magic states. These resource states are consumed by Steane-QEC or Knill-QEC teleportation gadgets, which simultaneously perform quantum error correction. Any quantum algorithm with $T$-depth $D_T$ can be executed by consuming $12D_T$ or fewer resource states. In particular, we find that the concatenated cultivation offers at least one order of magnitude spacetime reduction compared to cultivation in the surface code.

Problem-aware sampling for bias and variance reduction in randomised Hamiltonian simulation

Brian Coyle, Jonathan H. Fetherolf, Antonio Márquez Romero, Stefano Scali, Giuseppe Buonaiuto, Josh Kirsopp

Randomised quantum simulation algorithms such as qDRIFT reduce circuit depth by replacing deterministic product formulas with stochastic compilations, but achieving high accuracy can require many steps. In vanilla qDRIFT, Hamiltonian terms are sampled with probabilities proportional to their coefficient magnitudes. We propose StateDRIFT, a variant of qDRIFT which optimises its sampling distribution to estimate time-evolved quantities using knowledge of the initial state and target observable, with a provable truncation bound that keeps qDRIFT's guarantees. The distribution at each depth is chosen by solving an optimisation problem targeting bias and/or variance. StateDRIFT can significantly reduce the bias and variance of estimators, and hence circuit depth, which we test on short-time dynamics of lattice models and molecules under Jordan-Wigner up to 18 qubits. For example, with BeH2 StateDRIFT can reduce the number of two-qubit gates required to achieve a given accuracy from 11,201 to 983. StateDRIFT involves measuring observables and commutators on the initial state, so we propose two practical ways to estimate these: classical surrogates such as tensor networks, and classical shadows, which recover most or all of the saving from exact oracles in the systems tested.

Numerical studies separate mapping correctness, ansatz expressibility, preprocessing, Pauli growth, finite-shot uncertainty, and damage localization.
