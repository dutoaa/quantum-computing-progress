Sign-hedged Beam QAOA:Scaling Quantum Optimization Algorithms

Anupama Ray, Arush Sharma

Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA) encodes a combinatorial objective as an Ising Hamiltonian and alternates cost and mixer operations for p rounds. Recursive QAOA (RQAOA) scales further by using the resulting correlations, whether variables tend to agree or disagree, to eliminate variables one at a time. But each inferred sign is accepted irrevocably, so a single early mistake propagates through every later reduction and can discard the global optimum. As elimination proceeds, residuals shrink while effective edge density typically rises, so later decisions face denser Ising problems, a regime that fully connected Sherrington Kirkpatrick (SK) instances stress test directly. This paper proposes sign-hedged beam RQAOA, which retains the variable or pair selected by standard RQAOA, explores both possible sign assignments, keeps the best B residual problems according to a classical score, and completes each survivor with ordinary RQAOA. On frustrated SK instances, where standard recursion collapses to AR 0.474, beam recovers near-optimal AR 0.998 at the same p. We evaluate the workflow on 50+ instances from HamLib and QOBLIB, spanning 4 and 5 regular and Erdos Renyi MaxCut graphs, Sherrington Kirkpatrick (SK) spin glasses, and cardinality-constrained portfolio optimization, with up to 60 qubits and hardware runs on ibm kingston and ibm marrakesh. Matched simulator and device cells show consistent beam rankings: simulation and hardware agree that beam recovers near-optimal approximation ratio (AR) where standard recursion fails. These gains come from additional residual-circuit evaluations, classical pruning, and exact solution of a small core, rather than from increasing p, beam is a decision-side optimization atop fixed-p QAOA/RQAOA, not a claim that quantum circuits alone scale with qubit count.

QASPA-MaxCut on Unweighted Graphs: Edge Encoding and QAOA Restarts

Dmytro Sapozhnyk

Max-Cut is a common testbed for near-term quantum optimization because it has a simple Ising Hamiltonian, established classical baselines, and a parameterized quantum circuit whose performance depends on the outer classical optimizer. This paper develops QASPA-MaxCut, a shift-phase circuit for Max-Cut that encodes graph edges through controlled $R_y$ rotations of the form $\mathrm{CX}_{ij}R_y(θ_{ij})\mathrm{CX}_{ij}$. We derive the exact two-qubit algebra of the edge block, trace an unweighted four-vertex example, and compare one-layer QASPA-MaxCut with depth-one QAOA by exact state-vector simulation. The protocol uses seed 42, evaluates connected backbone-augmented random graphs with additional-edge probabilities $p_{\mathrm{edge}}\in\{0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7\}$, and adds nineteen custom unweighted topologies. The QASPA-MaxCut score is reported after the algorithm's classical selection stage: the four leading QASPA bit strings are evaluated by the Max-Cut objective, and the best cut is retained. The QAOA score is the expected approximation ratio after local optimization, reported as a best-of-$k$ curve over one to ten restarts. In these small exact simulations, fixed-angle QASPA-MaxCut produces reported group-mean selection ratios between $0.955$ and $1.000$, the topology-level readout is unchanged in the retained finite-shot checks under the rule $\lceil 0.03047\cdot 2^n+1016.2\rceil$, and depth-one QAOA improves with additional classical restarts while remaining lower than the QASPA selection score in the reported mean summaries. This comparison motivates further study of the classical search budget needed to obtain competitive Max-Cut samples at shallow circuit depth.

Sergey Shirobokov, Ben Jaderberg, Katerina Hristova, George Tsoukalas, Johannes Bausch, Eric Wieser

The timeline by which quantum computers can solve breakthrough problems in science depends on improved quantum algorithms as much as on better hardware. Such algorithmic advances are typically achieved through many years of expert human collaboration. Arrival-time problem in the quantum information era - observation of a Zeno crossover in monitored arrival-time distributions

Yousef Mafi, K. G. S. H. Gunawardana, Kim Pöyhönen, Teemu Ojanen

When does a quantum particle arrive at a detector? The temporal distributions of itinerant quantum particles have been debated since the birth of quantum mechanics. Here we study the arrival-time problem in periodically monitored spin chains and quantum circuits. The arrival dynamics exhibits two regimes, the quantum Zeno and the low-monitoring regime, separated by an optimal monitoring period set by the single-particle bandwidth. In support of the theory, on superconducting quantum hardware, we measure arrival-time distributions for single and multiple interacting particles in one- and two-dimensional circuits. The measured distributions resolve both regimes and agree closely with quantum-trajectory theory. The crossover persists in interacting many-particle systems: the optimal monitoring point is preserved while many-body interference reshapes the distributions. Zeno suppression likewise persists in two-dimensional geometry. Together, these results show that the two regimes are a generic feature of monitored propagation rather than a single-particle effect. To our knowledge, our work constitutes the first experimental measurement of arrival-time distributions in a monitored quantum transport setting, addressing one of the longest-standing problems in quantum mechanics.

Resource-efficient formulations of the Knapsack problem for quantum computing

Pim van den Heuvel, Vrinda Mehta, Dennis Willsch, J. A. Montanez-Barrera, Kristel Michielsen

Quantum computing has emerged as a new tool for solving combinatorial optimization problems. Quantum optimization approaches usually operate on the subclass of Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization (QUBO) problems. Many optimization problems can be reformulated in this form, though there are often multiple ways to do so. The choice of formulation can considerably affect the performance of QUBO solvers in practice. In this work, we study QUBO formulations of the Knapsack problem, a canonical example of a problem with inequality constraints. In the standard formulation these constraints are converted into penalty terms, which requires additional slack variables. Here, we investigate three more resource-efficient approaches for formulating the Knapsack as a QUBO. The first two modify the standard formulation, either by deriving a smaller penalty constant or by reducing the number of slack variables. The third approach is a wholly different method, which avoids the use of slack variables entirely. All three approaches increase the probability of finding the optimal solution, both in simulations of the linear ramp Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (LR-QAOA) and on a D-Wave quantum annealer. The slackless method performs best, increasing success probabilities by several orders of magnitude even for relatively small problem sizes.

We identify priorities for controlled benchmarking, noise-aware learning, reproducible evaluation, and efficient transfer between quantum and classical models.

Semi-Analytical Geometric Synthesis of Robust Quantum Controls

Eduardo Willwock Lussi, Rafael de Santiago, Eduardo Inacio Duzzioni, Hector Bessa Silveira

High-fidelity quantum gates are a central requirement for fault-tolerant quantum computation, yet their implementation is limited by the sensitivity of qubits to environmental noise and by control non-idealities. To mitigate these effects, quantum gates can be designed as dynamically corrected gates (DCGs), where control sequences are engineered to suppress the impact of errors on the resulting operation. Many methods in the literature are able to synthesize DCGs, but they typically rely on numerical optimization, with synthesis times on the order of minutes per gate. Here, we propose an automated semi-analytical method that reduces this cost by orders of magnitude, synthesizing smooth, robust control pulses in tens of milliseconds rather than minutes, with comparable fidelity and robustness. Our approach is based on the Space Curve Quantum Control (SCQC) formalism, where quantum evolution is mapped onto a geometric space curve whose geometric properties determine the target gate and its robustness. We parameterize the curve in the Frenet-Serret frame and employ the Pontryagin Maximum Principle (PMP) to design controls that are robust against both amplitude and dephasing errors. The PMP is formulated as a boundary-value problem and solved using a shooting method. The Frenet-Serret frame is integrated using Rodrigues' formula. Starting from a reference solution, we gradually vary the target rotation angle to construct a library of solutions, whose interpolation provides initial guesses for the shooting parameters of new target gates. We evaluated the method on 2,000 single-qubit rotations, obtaining a mean local optimization time of 39.945 ms per gate after preparation of the interpolation base. Quantum propagation yielded nominal gate infidelities below 10^-9 after virtual phase correction, with verified robustness against quasistatic amplitude and dephasing errors.

Scalable Unitary State-Encoding for Generic Large Data

Vittorio Pagni, Peter K. Schuhmacher, Michael Felderer, Michael Epping, Kevin Lively

Here, we introduce deterministic and scalable amplitude-encoding methods with low two-qubit gate costs and tunable trade-offs between circuit depth and encoding fidelity. Our approaches require no amplitude amplification or mid-circuit measurement and are capable of encoding arbitrary complex data or queryable functions by using tensor-networks to go beyond what can be held in classical memory. Our principle contributions are two complementary algorithms for simplifying fully controlled multiplexer circuits to one- and two-qubit gates. In the first, we formally map the elimination of redundant gate controls to a Boolean logic minimization problem, while in the second we utilize a single ancilla qubit to map the multi-controlled rotation gates to a sequence of one- and two-qubit operations via the Walsh-Hadamard spectrum of the gate angles. Hybridizing our methods gives up to orders of magnitude improvement in circuit depth at equivalent state fidelity compared to the default state-preparation method in the Qiskit package. We furthermore demonstrate our method on IQM hardware with a square lattice connectivity, finding excellent fidelity, even with minimal noise mitigation. By significantly reducing gate depth for encoding, this work enables the experimental execution of applications on current and near-term hardware which would otherwise be reduced to noise before the first calculation step, shortening the time horizon to robust quantum utility.
