Soft Mixture of Quantum Experts for Hybrid Quantum-Classical Learning

Valeria Repetto, Romina D'Aurizio, Piero Farinelli

Quantum Machine Learning (QML) has attracted increasing interest as a possible route to construct feature representations that are difficult to reproduce with classical models. However, near-term QML architectures based on trainable parametrized quantum circuits remain affected by limited hardware resources, noise, and severe optimization difficulties such as barren plateaus. In this work we propose Soft-MoQE, a Soft Mixture of Quantum Experts architecture that replaces variational quantum training with the input-dependent selection and combination of fixed quantum feature maps. The model is composed of a heterogeneous pool of non-trainable quantum circuits, each defining a distinct embedding, and a classical gating network that learns how to weight these embeddings for each data point. We benchmark Soft-MoQE on three PCA-reduced tasks: MNIST parity, MNIST 10-class, and FashionMNIST. Soft-MoQE outperforms the single best expert, the uniform average of all experts and a linear baseline, achieving task-dependent performance comparable to that of a trainable quantum neural network, without optimizing any quantum parameter and with a quantum training cost that is lower by orders of magnitude. The analysis of the class- conditioned expert usage shows that the learned routing combines a few experts for each input, with a selection that depends on the class. These results support the learned routing among fixed quantum circuits as a trainability-aware design principle for hybrid QML.

Ising-Encoded Computational Homogenization: Stress as a Quantum Observable

Mourad Halla, Suvranu De

Computational homogenization requires repeated solutions of representative volume element (RVE) problems to determine the effective response transferred to the macroscale, including the homogenized stress. Here, we develop a basis-encoded, energy-based variational quantum formulation for linear-elastic computational homogenization. The reduced finite-element energy of the RVE is expressed as a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) problem and mapped to an Ising Hamiltonian, whose ground state represents the equilibrium fluctuation field on the encoding grid. Using the Hellmann--Feynman theorem, differentiation of the strain-dependent Hamiltonian with respect to the prescribed macroscopic strain yields stress observables whose expectation values recover the conventional volume-averaged homogenized stress. A key structural result is that, although the equilibrium Hamiltonian is two-local, the stress observables are one-local. In linear elasticity, the two-qubit coupling coefficients are independent of macroscopic strain, hence, differentiation eliminates all two-qubit coupling terms, leaving only identity and single-qubit Pauli-$Z$ operators. These observables are diagonal in the same computational basis as the Ising Hamiltonian and can therefore be evaluated from the same computational-basis measurements used for the energy. Consequently, the homogenized stress can be obtained directly from the optimized quantum state using the same computational basis measurements as the Ising Hamiltonian, without requiring reconstruction of the microscopic displacement field or an additional measurement basis. Ground states are approximated using the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE), and the formulation is demonstrated on a two-dimensional periodic RVE.

Parallel transversal entangling gates for fault-tolerant trapped-ion quantum computing

Katrin Bolsmann, Alan Kahan, Alejandro Bermúdez, Markus Müller

Fault-tolerant quantum computing requires high-fidelity entangling gates that can be implemented efficiently across multiple qubit pairs. We present a scheme for simultaneous pairwise entangling operations, i.e., a transversal controlled-$Z$ gate, across neighboring wells in a double-well trapped-ion architecture. A tailored dynamical decoupling scheme suppresses unwanted couplings with a pulse count that scales quadratically with the number of ions. We show that incorporating a genuine geometric cancellation reduces the pulse count and, for selected register sizes, allows the parallel operation to be completed faster than an equivalent sequence of two-qubit gates while requiring a significantly smaller two-photon Rabi frequency. We assess the experimental feasibility of the protocol by analyzing the impact of decoherence and crosstalk arising from laser wave-vector misalignment. To gain insight into the robustness of fault-tolerant protocols against realistic imperfections of the proposed transversal gate scheme, we investigate how crosstalk-induced gate errors affect the fault-tolerant preparation of a logical Bell state between two $[[4,1,2]]$ quantum-error-detection code blocks. These results highlight the potential of transversal entangling gates as a building block for trapped-ion-based scalable fault-tolerant quantum error correction.

CAVQS: Context-Aware Variational Quantum Circuit Synthesis via Unitary Redistribution and Dynamic Reconstruction

Zhilong Liu, Xiangzhen Zhou, Yunqi Huang, Xin Hong, Pengcheng Zhu, Fanxu Meng

Variational quantum circuit synthesis jointly searches circuit structure and continuous parameters to reduce costly two-qubit gates, but block-based workflows typically instantiate blocks independently, limiting simplification across block boundaries. Moreover, fully unitary synthesis does not exploit opportunities created by permitted output measurements and classical feedforward. We present CAVQS, a context-aware variational synthesis framework that combines unitary redistribution and dynamic reconstruction. First, CAVQS uses QR decomposition to transfer unitary factors between overlapping blocks, preserving the overall transformation while reshaping local synthesis targets to enable cross-block reductions. Second, for blocks that precede permitted terminal measurements, CAVQS constructs ancilla-free dynamic implementations with a CNOT-budgeted pre-measurement search and analytic branch-unitary construction, thereby minimizing expected CNOT cost. Across random-circuit targets, CAVQS reduces mean two-qubit gate counts by 28.79%-42.52%, 2.05%-52.62%, and 30.51%-74.64% relative to QFAST, SQUANDER, and Fast-USYN, respectively, on common successful instances. On 4--6-qubit quantum algorithm benchmarks, the corresponding reductions are 27.27%-36.77%, 20.65%-69.10%, and 36.31%-71.88%, respectively.

A measurement-free quantum error correction protocol for superconducting cavities

Q. Rumman Rahman, S. M. Girvin, D. K. Weiss

The realization of large-scale quantum computation will require quantum error correction (QEC). One way to reduce the demands placed on QEC is to correct dominant physical errors at a lower level before passing the remaining errors to an outer code. Recently, Krastanov et al. [Nat. Commun. 12, 191 (2021)] proposed a measurement-free error-correction protocol based on coherent control of photonic modes that corrects for photon loss. Here, we adapt this approach to a circuit quantum electrodynamics (cQED) architecture using three superconducting microwave cavities coupled by Josephson-junction-based nonlinear elements. We encode the logical qubit in the lowest-order binomial code of one cavity, while the two other cavities serve as ancillas. Using numerical simulations with experimentally realistic parameters, we demonstrate that our protocol can correct single-photon loss with 99% fidelity. Furthermore, we optimize the waiting time between successive rounds of error-correction, and show that the logical lifetime can be extended by a factor of 1.2-1.8 relative to the unprotected Fock $\{|0\rangle, |1\rangle\}$ encoding, surpassing break-even performance. Our work highlights the potential of gate-based, measurement-free QEC for fault-tolerant quantum computing.

To harness the computational potential of existing fault-tolerant quantum platforms, we introduce topological quantum reservoir computing, a novel approach using protected degrees of freedom for storage and the host system's intrinsic dynamics for information processing. Using a one-dimensional Kitaev chain, we show how long-lived Majorana edge modes and fast bulk responses support these complementary functions within the same many-body system. We identify the tradeoffs governing access to protected memory and show how matched interfaces enable programmable writing and interactions enable autonomous information consolidation from the bulk to the edge. These mechanisms improve performance on tasks requiring long-term retention of temporal information. Our results establish topology as a new resource for quantum reservoir computing and open a route toward near-term quantum computing and quantum machine learning.

Quantum Annealing with a Quantum Neural Network: Width of Annealing Matters

Mojtaba Asadollahi, Danial Khajehzadeh, Abolfazl Ramezanpour

Quantum annealing is a promising quantum algorithm for dealing with computationally hard optimization problems. Currently available hardware can implement the algorithm for a significant class of Hamiltonian problems. However, its performance is especially limited in discontinuous quantum phase transitions, where the energy gap shrinks exponentially with system size. In this paper, we introduce an extension of quantum annealing to a quantum neural network and study its performance in a number of prototypical optimization problems. In particular, we observe that already a random quantum annealing network of width W = 2 can exponentially reduce the number of evolution layers or depth of the annealing process in the quantum search problem. By tuning the network parameters the search problem can be solved with exponentially small errors by a quantum perceptron of depth L = 1 and width W = 2. The findings show that width of a quantum annealing network is critical to enhance the success probability of the algorithm by providing a larger and learnable evolution space for the annealing process.

QuFrame: Pauli Frame Tracking for Fault-Tolerant Quantum Control

Aleksandra Świerkowska, Ilgaz Er, Emmanouil Giortamis, Pramod Bhatotia

Fault-tolerant quantum computing requires continuous quantum error correction (QEC) to suppress physical noise. While Clifford-only workloads can defer corrections to software post-processing, universal computation breaks this paradigm: executing non-Clifford gates via magic-state gadgets introduces hard, real-time control-flow dependencies on error-corrected measurements. Consequently, the controller must extract syndromes, decode them, and update the Pauli frame in real time, closing the entire QEC loop within a strict $1\,μ\text{s}$ cycle. However, prior proposals on Pauli frame tracking remain largely theoretical, leaving the microarchitectural bottlenecks of tracking, resolving, and synchronizing frame updates across distributed control pipelines unaddressed. To this end, we present QuFrame, a control microarchitecture for real-time Pauli frame tracking that closes the QEC loop within $1\,μ\text{s}$ while sustaining QPU-rate throughput. By introducing QuFrameISA, we cleanly decouple instruction execution from continuous Pauli frame management. Execution is distributed across per-qubit Instruction Resolution Engines (IREs) that resolve instructions against the frame via a single table lookup, while a Pauli Frame Management Unit enables independent, lock-free concurrent commits. To strictly guarantee the $1\,μ\text{s}$ deadline, QuFrame pairs an asynchronous primary decoder with an on-chip fallback decoder that resolves in $144\,\text{ns}$, natively reconciling divergent updates in hardware without degrading logical error rates.

Fault-tolerant quantum processor with minimal external control

Christopher A. Pattison, Jeongwan Haah, John Preskill

Contemporary classical information processors integrate tens of billions of transistors printed on a chip, yet are fully controlled through comparatively few external wires. This is possible because data flows through hard-coded operations in step with a global clock and is steered by external instructions. Today's solid-state quantum processors are different -- every qubit receives instructions from outside the chip via its own dedicated control line, and error correction adds a further torrent of syndrome data that is transported off-chip. Here we show that the external control of a quantum chip can be greatly simplified. We propose a fault-tolerant architecture for universal quantum computation, inspired by modern classical processors, in which quantum information flows through a fixed, clocked device, propelled by local gates in a planar array. Our quantum processor receives an input stream of classical instructions via a small, constant number of external wires, each attached to a single qubit. Except for those few qubits coupled to external wires, all qubits evolve under a periodically-applied geometrically-local depth-3 circuit, which is fixed at the time the device is fabricated. Assuming a noiseless global clock (so that every operation is applied on schedule, though imperfectly), we establish an accuracy threshold theorem: For physical noise below a universal threshold -- afflicting every gate, measurement, and wire -- a chip with $n$ physical qubits can run any quantum algorithm that uses~$n/{\sf polylog}(n/ε)$ logical qubits, where $ε$ is the error rate per logical gate. Each logical gate takes time~$n/{\sf polylog}(n/ε)$; an alternative design, in which quantum operations remain strictly local and noisy, but on-chip wires broadcast noiseless classical signals instantaneously, reduces the time per logical gate to ${\sf polylog}(n/ε)$.

We first consider stationary dynamics using a Vanilla-QRL agent and then introduce a recurrent GRU-QRL architecture to track non-stationary dynamics. We further compare the proposed approach with a Quantum Graph Recurrent Neural Network (QGRNN) baseline and evaluate frozen policies on unseen trajectories. Results show that both approaches accurately reconstruct stationary Hamiltonians, while GRU-QRL provides substantially improved tracking under non-stationary dynamics. These results demonstrate the potential of recurrent QRL as an adaptive system-identification tool for monitoring time-varying quantum systems.

Scaling classical hardware for accurate real-time quantum error correction

Chenyang Jin, Susanne F. Yelin, Andi Gu

Fault-tolerant quantum computation requires a classical decoder fast enough to process syndrome measurements at the rate at which they are generated, and to return its decisions with low latency. This is most demanding for superconducting processors, whose fast syndrome cycles force a trade-off between decoding speed and accuracy. Decoders fast enough to keep up sacrifice accuracy, which must be compensated for with larger codes and thus additional physical qubits. Here we introduce Rapids, a streaming neural decoder that is both fast and accurate. We achieve this by co-designing the decoder with a field-programmable gate array (FPGA): the network is built from operations that map cheaply onto the device and trained in its integer arithmetic. For the distance-19 surface code, Rapids decodes each round of syndromes in under a microsecond on a single FPGA, fast enough for superconducting processors, and returns its decisions with the low latency that fault-tolerant algorithms require. Its logical error rate is 240 times lower than that of existing real-time hardware decoders. The same design extends to the color code and the [[144,12,12]] bivariate-bicycle code, on which our decoder is 16 times more accurate than the real-time Relay decoder. We further show that the decoder scales: spread over several FPGAs or hardened into custom silicon, it becomes faster, more accurate, or able to decode larger codes. Classical hardware thus becomes a further resource, alongside qubits, that can be scaled to improve fault-tolerant quantum computers.

Non-Binary Detector Error Models

Stefano Tinelli, Francisco Lázaro, Francisco García Herrero

A key challenge in fault-tolerant quantum error correction (QEC) is that the syndrome extraction circuits are themselves faulty. Such circuit-level noise is commonly described by a detector error model (DEM). In Stim and in almost all related works, DEMs are binary: every non-binary circuit error (e.g., CNOT gate), is decomposed into independent binary error mechanisms. In this work, we introduce non-binary DEMs, in which each fault location of the syndrome extraction circuit is modeled by one random variable over its original alphabet, e.g., the 16 two-qubit Pauli errors after a CNOT gate. We also propose a split-merge-regroup transformation that builds a compact non-binary DEM from any Clifford circuit by merging the error components that flip the same detectors and observables. Non-binary DEMs represent any Pauli channel exactly, whereas binary DEMs are exact only for channels that factorize into independent binary parts. When the channels factorize, we prove that binary and non-binary DEMs are equivalent under degenerate quantum maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) decoding, but not under quantum MAP decoding. Although the variables are non-binary, belief propagation (BP) still exchanges a single scalar message per edge. Our numerical results with surface, color and bivariate bicycle codes show that non-binary DEMs have two main advantages. First, their Tanner graphs have four to five times fewer edges, which is advantageous for hardware implementations. Second, their performance under BP, with and without OSD post-processing, is better than that of their binary counterparts, and BP with OSD runs up to $9.4\times$ faster in a CPU.

QFT-Based Hybrid PointNet-Quantum Neural Networks for LiDAR Point-Cloud Classification

Anurag Kulkarni, Nouhaila Innan, Muhammad Shafique

Hybrid quantum-classical learning architectures provide a promising framework for integrating quantum feature transformations into complex data-processing tasks. Among these transformations, the Quantum Fourier Transform (QFT) offers a structured approach to feature processing, motivating an investigation of how its circuit configuration influences predictive performance, quantum resource requirements, and noise robustness. We present a hybrid PointNet-quantum neural network architecture for LiDAR point-cloud classification, combining classical feature extraction, amplitude encoding, and a variational quantum circuit. Five Fourier-processing configurations are systematically evaluated: No-QFT, Full-QFT-After, QFT-no-swap, IQFT-After, and Trainable-QFT. Experiments on KITTI, nuScenes, and PandaSet across three random seeds assess classification performance, class-level behavior, circuit complexity, and sensitivity to the IBM FakeManilaV2 noise model. Under ideal simulation, IQFT-After achieves the highest weighted F1-score on KITTI (0.857 $\pm$ 0.033), compared with 0.838 $\pm$ 0.023 for No-QFT. Removing the final SWAP gates in the QFT circuit reduces circuit complexity while preserving the Pauli-Z measurement features up to a fixed permutation that can be absorbed by the classification head. Under quantum noise, the circuit ranking changes, with IQFT-After exhibiting greater performance degradation and No-QFT and Trainable-QFT showing stronger robustness. Per-class analysis further identifies dataset-dependent differences in minority-class performance. These results demonstrate that Fourier-processing configuration influences both classification behavior and noise sensitivity, highlighting the role of circuit structure and hardware-aware evaluation in hybrid quantum learning for three-dimensional point-cloud data.
