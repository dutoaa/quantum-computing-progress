Variational Corrections to Strong-Disorder Renormalization in the Two-Dimensional Random Transverse Field Ising Model on Quantum Hardware

Alina Joch, Benedikt Fauseweh

Strong-disorder renormalization-group (SDRG) methods provide an effective description of disordered quantum many-body systems by identifying the dominant couplings for the low energy physics. However, the resulting renormalization-group flow is approximate and neglects correlations beyond the RG description. Here, we investigate whether variational quantum optimization can be used to correct these approximations. Building on the emergent-coupling-based ansatz (ECBA) developed for the one-dimensional random Heisenberg model, we introduce a formulation adapted to the two-dimensional random transverse field Ising model. The SDRG flow determines the structure of the circuit, while variational optimization allows the state to deviate from the RG approximation. We study the resulting ansatz numerically and experimentally on an IQM superconducting quantum processor. For a 16-qubit system, we perform full variational optimization on the quantum processor, while a 49-qubit system is investigated using pre-optimized parameters, achieving a relative energy accuracy of 89% and 97%, respectively. Our results demonstrate that RG information can be translated into shallow, hardware-compatible quantum circuits while variational optimization enables corrections beyond the underlying RG approximation also for two-dimensional systems.

Generative Models using Multi-layer Quantum Extreme Learning Machines

Amir Azzam, M. Pilar Casado, Michele Grossi, Arnau Riera, Sofia Vallecorsa

Quantum reservoir computing (QRC) and the closely related quantum extreme learning machine (QELM) paradigm exploit the complex dynamics of an uncontrolled quantum system for machine learning, offering an alternative to variational quantum algorithms whose training is hindered by barren plateaus and costly circuit optimization. Because the quantum system itself is left fixed, learning reduces to fitting a linear readout of a set of measured observables, and no gradient is ever taken through the quantum device. In this work we repurpose this paradigm for generative modeling: a fixed, randomly initialized multi-layer QELM transforms a scalar latent variable into a rich vector of measured expectation values, and only a simple linear readout is trained to map these to samples of a target distribution. The readout is fitted by minimizing the maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) between generated and target samples. We show that this algorithm is capable of reproducing a range of one- and two-dimensional distributions. Moreover, encoding the class label on dedicated qubits enables conditional generation of different classes of distributions with the same linear readout. We show that this cannot be achieved by the linear readout alone, and that the reservoir dynamics play a role in the ability to distinguish the different classes of distributions. We also show that using a simple six-qubit reservoir one is able to generate handwritten digits from the Digits and MNIST datasets with high accuracy. Moreover, replacing the Haar-random dynamics by time evolution under several physical Hamiltonians leaves the performance unchanged, provided the evolution spreads the input across the register. Finally, we show that a classical random Fourier feature model of the same size performs equally well: at the sizes we study, the QELM offers no advantage over its classical counterpart.

Rate-1/2 Quantum LDPC Codes for Multi-tier Superconducting Hardware

Jiaxuan Zhang, Zhao-Yun Chen, Jia-Ning Li, Tian-Hao Wei, Qing-Yang Hou, Wei-Cheng Kong

Quantum error correction is essential for scalable quantum computing, while its resource overhead is governed in large part by the encoding rate, namely the number of logical qubits encoded per physical data qubit. Recent quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) constructions have entered the ultra-high-rate regime with encoding rates above 1/2, but their syndrome-extraction circuits generally rely on reconfigurable connectivity or long-range qubit transport, making them difficult to implement on fixed-connectivity superconducting hardware. Here we introduce Armillary codes, a family of ultra-high-rate qLDPC codes whose algebraic structure is generated by native iSWAP routing under hardware constraints. The construction jointly enforces stabilizer commutation and correct parallel syndrome extraction, while admitting radial and circumferential couplings in crossing-free routing layers. We construct explicit codes with rates above 1/2, stabilizer weight 12, Tanner-graph girth 6, and distances from 12 to 16, with layouts on 5 or 6 hardware tiers. Compared with the Gross and Mitten codes at the same distances, the Armillary codes achieve encoding rates about 6 and 2.5 times those of the Gross and Mitten codes, respectively, while maintaining lower hardware-complexity scores. In circuit-level simulations, the \([\![8688,4348,16]\!]\) code approaches the teraquop regime, achieving a logical error rate of \(1.62\times10^{-12}\) per logical qubit per round at a physical error rate of 0.1\%. These results show that the rate-\(1/2\) regime of qLDPC codes can be extended from reconfigurable platforms to superconducting hardware, providing a route toward low-overhead fault-tolerant quantum processors.

SCOPE: A Physical Execution Latency-Guided Quantum Circuit Compilation Framework

Bolin Zhang, Zhiyuan Zhang, Yongguang Lv, Ruiqing He, Hengliang Guo, Jiandong Shang

Quantum computing requires compilers to translate algorithms represented in the quantum circuit model into unitary operations supported by physical devices. Limited coherence time makes physical execution latency an important compilation objective. Gate fusion can shorten pulses, but gate count and depth do not capture fused targets' control demands. Evaluating every candidate with quantum optimal control (QOC) would embed pulse optimization in structural search. We present Schmidt-guided Compilation for Optimized Pulse Execution (SCOPE), driven by an operator Schmidt time surrogate. The target's angular distance from product operators measures the required nonlocal change; dividing it by a coupling-limited evolution-rate bound gives a lower bound on exact implementation time. Trace-angle and waveform constraints complete block scores for critical-path evaluation during genetic search. Local QOC determines final pulse durations. Under a numerical nearest-neighbor control model, all 104 results across 13 instances from four application families have shorter QOC critical-path durations than an adapted efficient pulse generation framework with advanced synthesis for quantum circuits (EPOC). Using eight-run instance means, the equally weighted reduction is 42.50\%, with a geometric mean speedup of 2.00. In a shared six-qubit quantum Fourier transform (QFT) ablation, time-surrogate guidance reduces mean QOC duration by 22.21\% relative to gate-count guidance and 21.01\% relative to depth guidance, validating the surrogate's joint use in fitness evaluation and mutation-region selection.

Benchmarking memory and nonlinear processing capacity of quantum recurrent neural networks for time series prediction

José Daniel Viqueira, Andrés Gómez, David Mera

Quantum machine learning has emerged as a promising approach to time series prediction. While quantum reservoir computing has been widely explored for this setting, variational quantum models tailored to sequential data remain comparatively understudied, largely because they are difficult to train. In this work, we present a rigorous benchmark of variational quantum recurrent neural networks (QRNNs) on time series prediction tasks, focusing on two key capabilities: memory and nonlinear processing. We introduce a task-driven methodology that quantifies model capacity, enabling an intuitive and fair comparison across architectures, including classical recurrent baselines. We evaluate the proposed framework on two generic benchmark tasks and on a synthetic dataset describing a chaotic system. Overall, the quantum models achieve performance comparable to their classical counterparts, but classical models scale better with the memory size within the range studied. Notably, one QRNN variant shows higher average capacity on the task designed to jointly probe memory and nonlinear capacity. Moreover, our results show that quantum models offer better capacity when we are able to find a good parameter initialization. However, the increasing sensitivity to initialization leads to a lower probability of finding good solutions as the model size increases. These results provide a principled starting point for designing richer quantum recurrent architectures and for assessing where they may offer practical benefits.

Quantum sine imaginary-time evolution: sequential and QSVT implementations of a probabilistic ground-state filter

Victor A. Penas, Tamara Bottazzi, Marcelo Losada, Diego Tielas, Federico Holik

We propose quantum sine imaginary-time evolution (QSITE), a quantum algorithm for preparing ground states of a Hamiltonian. QSITE approximates imaginary-time evolution through an exact sine filter. We give two circuit implementations of this operator: a sequential one that applies it as a concatenation of linear combinations of unitaries (LCU), reusing two ancilla qubits across steps via mid-circuit measurement, and a coherent one based on the quantum singular value transformation (QSVT). A telescoping identity shows the two prepare the same state and succeed with identical probability, differing only in how that probability can be boosted. QSITE belongs to the family of ancilla-based probabilistic imaginary-time filters and we state rigorous convergence guarantees: we prove that the QSITE operator converges uniformly to the exact ITE operator, that the prepared state converges to the ideal ITE state, and that the filter probability increases monotonically with each concatenation step. Although the construction applies to an arbitrary Hamiltonian, we specialize it to optimization Hamiltonians, and this specialization is what exposes its structure more easily. Since MaxCut eigenvalues are integers, the probability of failure is periodic in the evolution time, yielding safe zones, the first located analytically and the second numerically. For MaxCut we further show that, when the tolerated energy error is a fixed fraction of the ground-state energy, the probability of failure can be bounded below 1/2 with concatenation steps growing linearly with the number of qubits in the first safe zone. We present numerical results for random Erdös-Rényi instances with up to 24 qubits and benchmark QSITE against QAOA.

Resolving and Suppressing Temporally Correlated Noise in Quantum Error Correction

Alec Eickbusch, Nick Noll, Michael Newman, Matt McEwen, Leigh S. Martin, Alexis Morvan

Scaling fault-tolerant quantum error correction (QEC) requires suppressing errors in both space and time, yet standard logical memory experiments primarily probe spatial faults. Here, we use a 105-qubit Willow superconducting quantum processor to realize and scale the surface code stability experiment, which is alternatively sensitive to temporal error chains. Scaling from patch sizes of $3 \times 3$ to $7 \times 7$, we realize a timelike block suppression factor of $μ_T = 1.47 \pm 0.25$, demonstrating the ability to maintain temporal error suppression as the number of qubits increases. By extending the stability experiment for many cycles, we uncover a sum-of-exponentials behavior in error suppression caused by a temporally correlated error: data-qubit heating to the third excited state $|3\rangle$. We fully capture this effect with a parameter-free, device-level leakage model incorporating higher-state transport during syndrome extraction cycles. To suppress this error, we develop and realize a walking stability circuit, reaching a logical error probability floor of $\le 3.20\times 10^{-8}$ at 60 cycles---two orders of magnitude below the standard construction---and limited by ionizing radiation error bursts. Finally, by adaptively terminating syndrome extraction based on decoder confidence in an offline analysis, we reduce spacetime logical error by 38% and time overhead by 34% compared to the optimal fixed-cycle strategy.

Specifying & Verifying Quantum Error-Correcting Codes

Stavan Jain, Nithin Raveendran, Aws Albarghouthi

Building quantum computers is challenging due to the noise that affects qubits. Quantum error correction tackles noise by encoding information in quantum error-correcting (QEC) codes. These codes are often complex, increasingly found by computational search, presented in diverse formalisms with informal arguments, and hard to reason about algorithmically. Given that the rest of the quantum computing stack relies on a QEC code's properties for correct computation, we propose a formally verified foundation for QEC codes. We present QECLean, a Lean-based framework for formally specifying and verifying QEC codes. QECLean backs every claimed QEC-code property with a machine-checked proof, unifies the diverse presentations of codes in the literature through common abstractions, and makes new codes easy to specify. We present a new compositional technique, code combinators, for proving code distance analytically. Code combinators allow us to prove the distance of large codes that are out of reach for algorithmic techniques; for example, we show that IBM's $[[360, 12, \leq 24]]$ code has distance 24, which was previously only known as an upper bound. Furthermore, we show how our approach allows us to formalize and prove entire code families, such as the surface code. Our work lays the foundation required for reasoning about circuit-level implementations of codes on given architectures and for building end-to-end verified compilers for quantum computers. QECLean is available at https://github.com/Stavan-Jain/QECLean

Without retuning, the gross-code parameters yield fewer observed failures than Relay-BP-5 in its published gross-code configuration across four additional code families. Ablations support state-dependent memory control, while a loop-gain analysis characterizes specific amplification modes in the decoding dynamics.

These results provide a framework for predicting annealing bottlenecks, and demonstrate Hilbert-space engineering as a strategy for improving neutral-atom quantum optimization protocols.

Pulse-timing errors in exchange-only spin qubits: how one control imperfection propagates to VQE, QAOA and fault-tolerance primitives

Yuichiro Minato

In an exchange-only (EO) spin qubit every gate is a sequence of Heisenberg exchange pulses whose only continuous parameter is the pulse area. We ask how an error in that area propagates to an algorithm's result. On up to six encoded qubits we compile VQE for H$_2$ and LiH, QAOA MaxCut, Grover search, phase estimation and a distance-3 repetition code into exchange pulses and perturb every pulse with a systematic fractional error $\varepsilon$, an offset or per-pulse jitter. For circuits whose first-order response is negligible at the threshold, the useful regime ends at $\varepsilon$ of a few $10^{-3}$: chemical accuracy is lost at $\approx6\times10^{-3}$ for both molecules and 5% of success probability between 3 and $9\times10^{-3}$ for 498-1216 pulses. A systematic error accumulates as the square of the depth and biases the answer; jitter accumulates linearly and, within statistics, does not bias phase estimation. Leakage from inter-block pulses is a third of the CNOT error, survives variational re-optimisation and a leakage-controlled entangler, and makes a leakage-blind repetition-code round worse than its uncorrected data by a factor 38-93 at $\varepsilon=10^{-2}$ in three of four compilations; in the fourth the uncorrected data already fail. The compilation moves the thresholds by a factor of two to eight. Measured over-rotation and exchange noise put devices at the edge of this regime, and with the measured 3.5 $μ$s $T_2^*$ a quasi-static magnetic gradient limits thousand-pulse circuits unless $J/h$ reaches several hundred MHz with continuous areas, because a duration grid of 2-5% of the SWAP time starts to cost chemical accuracy. As a bit budget, a common-mode area error needs 8-10 significant bits and a 6-bit significand for the durations suffices in the tested circuits; half-precision words and picosecond timers exceed this; older devices had 6-8 bits of analog stability.

We develop a circuit for preparing a U(1)~$\times$~SU(2)-symmetric MPS directly from spin-adapted reduced tensors. Using Householder--LKS synthesis, the circuit prepares each symmetry-allowed reduced block and applies Clebsch--Gordan coefficients to recover the magnetic degrees of freedom, avoiding explicit loading of the corresponding unfolded dense tensors. For real-amplitude circuits built from half-angle rotations, the classical Fisher information equals the quantum Fisher information almost everywhere, so its trace equals the parameter count and carries no information about fit quality. The Fisher eigenvalue rank counts the live nodes of the amplitude tree: it coincides with the label on a sparse benchmark and is uninformative on a full-support one. The pooled Wasserstein and maximum-mean-discrepancy scores move with optimizer convergence and with the scoring convention. For two bitstring defect families on which both metrics have closed forms, we compute the tolerance-mismatch window in which the discrepancy rejects generators that the transport threshold admits; it opens only at certification-grade tolerances and closes as the qubit number grows. On the Hamming cost we prove a sharp tolerance ratio above which the discrepancy vote is redundant, and we obtain the exact worst-parity minimax value over all unit-diagonal positive-semidefinite kernels, which is exponentially small in the qubit number. In a numerical campaign at the tolerances a near-term experiment can resolve, the discrepancy vote changed no verdict at a thousand shots or more. Static Bounds on Thermal Fast Scrambling

Yixuan Ma, Weikang Li, Dong-Ling Deng

Scrambling, the process by which initially localized quantum information spreads across an interacting many-body system, is a hallmark of quantum chaos and plays a central role in understanding black hole dynamics. In thermal systems, the chaos bound of Maldacena, Shenker and Stanford limits the exponential growth rate of out-of-time-order correlations to $2π/β$, assuming that time-ordered correlation functions factorize after a dissipation time. Establishing the corresponding rigorous logarithmic lower bounds on scrambling times at arbitrary fixed temperature has remained an open problem, most notably in disordered systems, where the Gibbs state depends on the same random couplings that drive the dynamics. Here we prove such bounds at every fixed temperature for the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model and for random $k$-local spin systems, without assuming real-time factorization. Our approach uses the operator-size distribution of the square root of the Gibbs state to quantify how local probes modify its structure. This static information bounds the thermal correlator uniformly in time along the edges of its analytic strip in complex time, and analyticity then limits how rapidly scrambling can develop. For both families of models, we prove that with probability tending to one the scrambling time is at least $(1-ε)[β/(2π)]\log N$ for any $0<ε<1$, with the coefficient $β/(2π)$ suggested by the chaos bound. The bound holds for each fixed pair of distinct Majorana modes in the SYK model and for the target average of any fixed single-site source in the spin model. These results place the thermal fast-scrambling timescale on a rigorous footing and show how static properties of the Gibbs state can constrain real-time information spreading.
